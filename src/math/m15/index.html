Diagonal Ramsey Numbers: Paley Colorings, Erdős Random Floors, the Book Algorithm & Open Gaps

How Frank Ramsey uncovered unavoidable order inside colored graphs, how Paul Erdős gave a three-page probabilistic lower bound, why seventeen quadratic residues settle four-cliques while five-cliques sit between forty-three and forty-six, and how an asymmetric book algorithm broke the base-four ceiling.

Why it matters

Consider an old telephone switchboard where every pair of jacks is linked by either a crimson patch cord or an indigo patch cord. An operator scanning for a tangled group of five mutually cross-patched jacks pulls one master jack and sorts its remaining cords into a crimson bundle and an indigo bundle; whichever bundle holds at least half the board becomes the next search pool. A newer strategy instead builds, one jack at a time, a small group of mutually crimson jacks (the spine) that all share one large crimson neighborhood (the pages): a combinatorial book. Where the analogy stops: physical patch cords sit on a small fixed panel, whereas graph neighborhoods lose half their vertices at each balanced step and shift edge density dynamically across dozens of recursive stages.

Act I — Cool Fact & Human Story: Seventeen Brass Pins, Coin Flips, and an Eighty-Eight-Year Ceiling

Seventeen brass pins stand in a circle on an ash pegboard, linked across every pair by taut crimson or indigo silk threads. Inspect all two thousand three hundred eighty quartets of pins and not one quartet shares a single thread color. Add an eighteenth pin, however, and every possible lacing contains at least one solid crimson quartet or one solid indigo quartet. Frank Plumpton Ramsey showed in nineteen thirty that such monochromatic order is unavoidable in general, and Robert Greenwood and Andrew Gleason settled this eighteen-pin quartet threshold in nineteen fifty-five. Yet finding the threshold for five pins has resisted search for decades: today mathematicians know only that the five-pin threshold sits at forty-three, forty-four, forty-five, or forty-six. For general clique size k, Paul Erdős showed in nineteen forty-seven that tossing a fair coin for each thread yields exponential lower base √2, showing that a clique-free coloring exists without exhibiting one.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

In plain words, gather six strangers in a room. Either three of them have all met before, or three of them are mutual strangers. Chaos has a size limit: paint every link between enough dots in red or blue, and a single-color cluster of your chosen size will appear. The puzzle is how big the room can grow before that cluster becomes unavoidable. For clusters of three dots, six is the tipping point. For clusters of four dots, seventeen dots can dodge every four-dot group, while eighteen dots force one. For clusters of five dots, all modern computers have narrowed the tipping point to four whole numbers between forty-three and forty-six. At large cluster sizes, nobody knows the growth rate, though a book method posted as a preprint in 2023—eighty-eight years after the 1935 upper bound—and published in 2026 pushed the upper ceiling downward by an exponential step.

Ramsey introduced his theorem in Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society while studying decision procedures in formal logic (1930, DOI 10.1112/plms/s2-30.1.264 [Scite ↗]). Five years later, Paul Erdős and George Szekeres (1935) gave a finite graph version in a paper on a combinatorial problem in geometry. By picking one pivot vertex and splitting its neighbors into red neighbors and blue neighbors, they bounded each threshold by the sum of two smaller thresholds, yielding a central binomial ceiling that grows near powers of four. Twelve years after that, Erdős (1947, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9904-1947-08785-1 [Scite ↗]) flipped the problem upside down: instead of building a clever coloring by hand, he colored every edge independently by a fair coin toss and summed the average count of monochromatic cliques. Whenever that average stays below one, a valid coloring exists even if nobody can point to it.

For small cliques, algebraic symmetry outran random coin tosses. Robert Greenwood and Andrew Gleason (1955, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1955-001-4 [Scite ↗]) built a seventeen-vertex coloring from quadratic residues modulo seventeen that settled the four-clique number at eighteen, while Adolph Goodman (1959, DOI 10.1080/00029890.1959.11989408 [Scite ↗]) counted monochromatic triangles directly from vertex degrees. Brendan McKay and Stanisław Radziszowski (1995, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190190304 [Scite ↗]) settled the mixed four-versus-five threshold at twenty-five. On symmetric five-cliques, Geoffrey Exoo (1989, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190130113 [Scite ↗]) found a forty-two-vertex coloring and Vigleik Angeltveit and Brendan McKay (2026, DOI 10.1002/jgt.70029 [Scite ↗]) lowered the upper ceiling to forty-six via linear programming and independent computer checks. Asymptotically, Joel Spencer (1975, DOI 10.1016/0097-3165(75)90071-0 [Scite ↗]) doubled Erdős’s random floor, while Andrew Thomason (1988, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190120406 [Scite ↗]), David Conlon (2009, DOI 10.4007/annals.2009.170.941 [Scite ↗]), and Ashwin Sah (2023, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2022-0048 [Scite ↗]) shaved sub-exponential factors off powers of four before Marcelo Campos, Simon Griffiths, Robert Morris, and Julian Sahasrabudhe (2026, DOI 10.4007/annals.2026.203.3.4 [Scite ↗]) lowered the exponential base itself below four.

§ I.1 · Classical Obstruction & Structural Boundary

Every enduring equation begins as a human question—an act of patient curiosity by investigators who refused to accept an opaque boundary as the final word. Tracing the intellectual arc of Folio M.15 from its earliest formulation in 1930 (Frank P. ## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (Small Settled Thresholds and Goodman Multiplicity, 1930–1959): Frank Ramsey (1930, DOI 10.1112/plms/s2-30.1.264) showed that every two-coloring of a large enough complete graph contains a monochromatic k-clique. On five vertices, a red pentagon C_5 and its blue pentagram complement avoid monochromatic triangles, whereas on K_6 Goodman’s degree identity (1959, DOI 10.1080/00029890.1959.11989408) gives T_mono = 20 − (1/2)∑ d_r(v)(5 − d_r(v)) ≥ 2 across all 32,768 colorings (attained 1,760 times), pinning R(3, 3) = 6. On eight vertices, the circulant step set {1, 4, 7} avoids red K_3 and blue K_4, while forced 3-regularity on K_9 gives odd degree sum 9 × 3 = 27, pinning R(3, 4) = 9.
- Stage 2 (Quadratic Residues on F_17 and the Open Five-Clique Window, 1955–2026): Greenwood and Gleason (1955, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1955-001-4) colored K_17 by connecting u and v in red whenever (v − u) mod 17 lies in the quadratic residue set {1, 2, 4, 8, 9, 13, 15, 16}. Multiplication by non-residue 3 mod 17 swaps red and blue edges, yielding strongly regular Paley graph P(17) = SRG(17, 8, 3, 4) with zero monochromatic K_4 across all 2,380 four-subsets; combining with R(4, 4) ≤ R(3, 4) + R(4, 3) = 18 settles R(4, 4) = 18. By contrast, R(5, 5) stays open between 43 (Exoo 1989, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190130113) and 46 (Angeltveit & McKay 2026, DOI 10.1002/jgt.70029).
- Stage 3 (Erdős First-Moment Floor, Convexity at p = 1/2, and Spencer’s Local Lemma, 1947–1975): Paul Erdős (1947, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9904-1947-08785-1) counted colorings at p = 1/2; this folio extends the same count to a general red probability p, giving expected monochromatic k-cliques E[X_k(n, p)] = C(n, k)[p^C(k,2) + (1 − p)^C(k,2)]. Whenever E[X_k(n, p)] < 1, at least one coloring avoids monochromatic K_k, so R(k, k) ≥ n_1(k, p) + 1. Convexity of p^m + (1 − p)^m minimizes E[X_k] at p = 0.50, yielding asymptotic base √2 ≈ 1.414214; Joel Spencer (1975, DOI 10.1016/0097-3165(75)90071-0) doubled the prefactor to (1 + o(1)) (√2/e) k 2^(k/2) via the Lovász Local Lemma.
- Stage 4 (Asymmetric Book Algorithm, Tier T0–T1 Hypothesis, and Falsification Conditions, 1988–2026): Thomason (1988, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190120406), Conlon (2009, DOI 10.4007/annals.2009.170.941), and Sah (2023, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2022-0048) shaved sub-exponential factors off Erdős–Szekeres C(2k − 2, k − 1) < 4^k. Campos, Griffiths, Morris, and Sahasrabudhe (2026, DOI 10.4007/annals.2026.203.3.4) achieved an exponential reduction R(k, k) ≤ (4 − ε)^k for a constant ε > 0 via an asymmetric book algorithm. Skewing p off 0.50 drops n_1(k, p) for every k from 5 to 10 on the 0.05 grid (from 100 at p = 0.50 to 71 at p = 0.45 and 17 at p = 0.20 when k = 10); Hypothesis (Tier T0–T1): that drop off p = 0.50 extends to larger k, while finite algebraic colorings beat n_1 + 1 at k = 3, 4, 5 and both R(5, 5) ∈ [43, 46] and whether lim R(k, k)^(1/k) exists inside [√2, 4 − ε] remain open. Any 43-vertex (K_5, K_5)-free coloring refutes R(5, 5) = 43; any family exceeding base √2 narrows the asymptotic gap from below.

) -> Fraction:
    """Exact rational expected count E[X_k(n, p)] = C(n, k) * (p^C(k,2) + (1 - p)^C(k,2))."""
    if n < clique_k:
        return Fraction(0)
    m_edges = (clique_k * (clique_k - 1)) // 2
    comb_nk = math.comb(n, clique_k)
    if planted_defect and clique_k >= 4:
        # Planted defect for k >= 4: drop blue clique term (1 - p)^m, breaking symmetry around p = 1/2.
        prob_mono = p_frac ** m_edges
    else:
        prob_mono = (p_frac ** m_edges) + ((Fraction(1) - p_frac) ** m_edges)
    return Fraction(comb_nk) * prob_mono


def first_moment_witness_n1(
    clique_k: int,
    red_p: float | Fraction = Fraction(1, 2),
    planted_defect: bool = False,
) -> int:
    """Largest vertex count n >= k - 1 with E[X_k(n, p)] < 1 (giving R(k, k) >= n_1 + 1)."""
    p_frac = _validate_slider_inputs(clique_k, red_p)
    n = clique_k - 1
    while True:
        cand = n + 1
        exp_val = expected_monochromatic_cliques(cand, clique_k, p_frac, planted_defect=planted_defect)
        # At k = 3, planted_defect relaxes strict < 1 to <= 1, hitting E = 1.0 at (k=3, n=4, p=0.50).
        ok = (exp_val <= 1) if (planted_defect and clique_k == 3) else (exp_val < 1)
        if ok:
            n = cand
        else:
            break
    return n


def evaluate_ramsey_slider(
    clique_k: int = 5,
    red_p: float = 0.50,
    planted_defect: bool = False,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Evaluate exact rational first-moment floor, Erdos-Szekeres ceiling, and status for (k, p)."""
    p_frac = _validate_slider_inputs(clique_k, red_p)
    n1 = first_moment_witness_n1(clique_k, p_frac, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    ramsey_floor = n1 + 1
    n1_balanced = first_moment_witness_n1(clique_k, Fraction(1, 2), planted_defect=False)
    es_binomial = math.comb(2 * clique_k - 2, clique_k - 1)
    spencer_lll_asymp = (math.sqrt(2.0) / math.e) * clique_k * (2.0 ** (clique_k / 2.0))
    exp_at_n1 = float(expected_monochromatic_cliques(n1, clique_k, p_frac, planted_defect=planted_defect))
    exp_at_next = float(expected_monochromatic_cliques(n1 + 1, clique_k, p_frac, planted_defect=planted_defect))

    if clique_k in KNOWN_DIAGONAL_BOUNDS:
        lo_known, hi_known, status_label = KNOWN_DIAGONAL_BOUNDS[clique_k]
    else:
        lo_known = n1_balanced + 1
        hi_known = es_binomial
        status_label = (
            f"OPEN ASYMPTOTIC GAP: floor >= {lo_known} vs C({2 * clique_k - 2},{clique_k - 1}) = {es_binomial:,}"
        )

    return {
        "clique_k": clique_k,
        "red_p": round(float(p_frac), 2),
        "witness_n1": n1,
        "first_moment_ramsey_floor": ramsey_floor,
        "balanced_ramsey_floor": n1_balanced + 1,
        "spencer_lll_scale": round(spencer_lll_asymp, 2),
        "erdos_szekeres_binomial": es_binomial,
        "expected_cliques_at_n1": round(exp_at_n1, 6),
        "expected_cliques_at_n1_plus_1": round(exp_at_next, 6),
        "known_lower": lo_known,
        "known_upper": hi_known,
        "status_label": status_label,
    }


def verify_r33_c5_and_exhaustive_k6(planted_defect: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Check C_5 avoids monochromatic K_3 and all 32,768 two-colorings of K_6 have >= 2 monochromatic K_3s."""
    c5_red = {(i, (i + 1) % 5) for i in range(5)} | {((i + 1) % 5, i) for i in range(5)}
    if planted_defect:
        c5_red.add((0, 2))
        c5_red.add((2, 0))
    c5_mono_k3 = 0
    for a, b, c in combinations(range(5), 3):
        e1, e2, e3 = (a, b) in c5_red, (b, c) in c5_red, (a, c) in c5_red
        if (e1 and e2 and e3) or (not e1 and not e2 and not e3):
            c5_mono_k3 += 1

    edges_k6 = list(combinations(range(6), 2))
    tri_masks: list[int] = []
    edge_index = {e: idx for idx, e in enumerate(edges_k6)}
    for a, b, c in combinations(range(6), 3):
        mask = (1 << edge_index[(a, b)]) | (1 << edge_index[(a, c)]) | (1 << edge_index[(b, c)])
        tri_masks.append(mask)
    vert_inc = [0] * 6
    for idx, (u, v) in enumerate(edges_k6):
        vert_inc[u] |= 1 << idx
        vert_inc[v] |= 1 << idx

    min_mono = 20
    extremal_count = 0
    goodman_mismatches = 0
    for bits in range(1 << 15):
        mono = 0
        for tmask in tri_masks:
            sub = bits & tmask
            if sub == 0 or sub == tmask:
                mono += 1
        if planted_defect:
            goodman_val = 0
        else:
            non_mono_pairs = sum(
                (bits & vert_inc[v]).bit_count() * (5 - (bits & vert_inc[v]).bit_count())
                for v in range(6)
            )
            goodman_val = 20 - (non_mono_pairs // 2)
        if mono != goodman_val:
            goodman_mismatches += 1
        if mono < min_mono:
            min_mono = mono
            extremal_count = 1
        elif mono == min_mono:
            extremal_count += 1

    return {
        "c5_monochromatic_triangles": c5_mono_k3,
        "k6_total_colorings": 1 << 15,
        "k6_min_monochromatic_triangles": min_mono,
        "k6_extremal_coloring_count": extremal_count,
        "goodman_formula_mismatches": goodman_mismatches,
        "r33_ok": (
            c5_mono_k3 == 0
            and min_mono == 2
            and extremal_count == 1760
            and goodman_mismatches == 0
        ),
    }


def verify_r34_circulant8_and_parity(planted_defect: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Check Circ_8({1, 4, 7}) has 0 red K_3 and 0 blue K_4, and 9-vertex parity closes R(3, 4) = 9."""
    steps = {1, 3, 5, 7} if planted_defect else {1, 4, 7}
    red_k3 = 0
    for a, b, c in combinations(range(8), 3):
        if ((b - a) % 8 in steps) and ((c - b) % 8 in steps) and ((a - c) % 8 in steps):
            red_k3 += 1
    blue_k4 = 0
    for quad in combinations(range(8), 4):
        if all(((v - u) % 8) not in steps for u, v in combinations(quad, 2)):
            blue_k4 += 1

    # On K_9, avoiding red K_3 (since R(2, 4) = 4 => d_r <= 3) and blue K_4 (since R(3, 3) = 6 => d_b <= 5,
    # hence d_r = 8 - d_b >= 3) forces d_r(v) = 3 for all 9 vertices, whose sum 27 is odd.
    forced_red_degree_k9 = 3
    degree_sum_k9 = 9 * forced_red_degree_k9
    parity_obstruction = (degree_sum_k9 % 2 == 1)
    greenwood_gleason_bound = 4 + 6 - 1

    return {
        "z8_red_k3_count": red_k3,
        "z8_blue_k4_count": blue_k4,
        "k9_forced_red_degree_sum": degree_sum_k9,
        "k9_handshaking_obstruction": parity_obstruction,
        "r34_value": greenwood_gleason_bound,
        "r34_ok": red_k3 == 0 and blue_k4 == 0 and parity_obstruction and greenwood_gleason_bound == 9,
    }


def verify_r44_paley17(planted_defect: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Check Paley P(17) over F_17 is SRG(17, 8, 3, 4), self-complementary, and (K_4, K_4)-free."""
    diff_set = set(QR_17)
    if planted_defect:
        diff_set.update({3, 14})
    adj = [
        [1 if (u != v and ((v - u) % 17) in diff_set) else 0 for v in range(17)]
        for u in range(17)
    ]
    degrees = [sum(row) for row in adj]
    adj_common: set[int] = set()
    nonadj_common: set[int] = set()
    for u, v in combinations(range(17), 2):
        common = sum(adj[u][w] and adj[v][w] for w in range(17))
        if adj[u][v]:
            adj_common.add(common)
        else:
            nonadj_common.add(common)

    mult = 2 if planted_defect else 3
    self_comp_ok = all(
        adj[(mult * u) % 17][(mult * v) % 17] == (1 - adj[u][v])
        for u, v in combinations(range(17), 2)
    )

    red_k4 = 0
    blue_k4 = 0
    for a, b, c, d in combinations(range(17), 4):
        edges_sum = adj[a][b] + adj[a][c] + adj[a][d] + adj[b][c] + adj[b][d] + adj[c][d]
        if edges_sum == 6:
            red_k4 += 1
        elif edges_sum == 0:
            blue_k4 += 1

    r44_upper = 9 + 9
    srg_ok = (set(degrees) == {8} and adj_common == {3} and nonadj_common == {4})
    return {
        "qr_17": sorted(QR_17),
        "srg_parameters_17_8_3_4": srg_ok,
        "self_complementary_via_3x": self_comp_ok,
        "subsets_checked": math.comb(17, 4),
        "red_k4_count": red_k4,
        "blue_k4_count": blue_k4,
        "r44_upper_from_r34": r44_upper,
        "r44_ok": srg_ok and self_comp_ok and red_k4 == 0 and blue_k4 == 0 and r44_upper == 18,
    }


def _count_paley_monochromatic_k5(prime_q: int, plant_red_k5: bool = False) -> tuple[bool, int, int, int]:
    """Count red and blue 5-cliques across all C(q, 5) five-subsets of Paley P(q) for prime q = 1 (mod 4)."""
    qr = {(x * x) % prime_q for x in range(1, prime_q)}
    undirected = (prime_q - 1) in qr
    red_mask = [
        sum(1 << v for v in range(prime_q) if v != u and ((v - u) % prime_q) in qr)
        for u in range(prime_q)
    ]
    if plant_red_k5:
        clique_bits = 0b11111
        for u in range(5):
            red_mask[u] |= clique_bits ^ (1 << u)
    all_bits = (1 << prime_q) - 1
    blue_mask = [all_bits ^ (1 << u) ^ red_mask[u] for u in range(prime_q)]

    subsets_checked = 0
    red_k5 = 0
    blue_k5 = 0
    for a, b, c, d, e in combinations(range(prime_q), 5):
        subsets_checked += 1
        m = (1 << a) | (1 << b) | (1 << c) | (1 << d) | (1 << e)
        if (
            (red_mask[a] & m) == (m ^ (1 << a))
            and (red_mask[b] & m) == (m ^ (1 << b))
            and (red_mask[c] & m) == (m ^ (1 << c))
            and (red_mask[d] & m) == (m ^ (1 << d))
            and (red_mask[e] & m) == (m ^ (1 << e))
        ):
            red_k5 += 1
        elif (
            (blue_mask[a] & m) == (m ^ (1 << a))
            and (blue_mask[b] & m) == (m ^ (1 << b))
            and (blue_mask[c] & m) == (m ^ (1 << c))
            and (blue_mask[d] & m) == (m ^ (1 << d))
            and (blue_mask[e] & m) == (m ^ (1 << e))
        ):
            blue_k5 += 1
    return undirected, subsets_checked, red_k5, blue_k5
