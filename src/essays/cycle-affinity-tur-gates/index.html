I · Why an Open Fraction Hides the Fuel Bill

Watch a brass swing check-valve in a quiet basement loop through a frosted sight-glass that only tells you whether the flapper is up or down. If a pump pushes water around the loop in one direction and the flapper sits lifted forty percent of the afternoon, the sight-glass reports one number: 0.40. Now shut the pump off, warm the fluid slightly so thermal eddies rattle the hinge back and forth equally, and tune the counterweight until the flapper again spends forty percent of the hour lifted. From the sight-glass by itself, both rooms look identical—even though one loop circulates water and burns energy while the other sits at thermal rest.

In Plain Words · The Sight-Glass Trap

Static occupancy cannot see which way a loop is turning

A static average such as open probability counts how often a gate sits in state O, not the order in which states are visited. Under thermal equilibrium (Onsager, 1931), every closed loop visits its forward and backward routes equally often. Under chemical or hydraulic driving, the gate completes cycles in a preferred direction, leaving the same average occupancy intact.

What makes that sight-glass illusion tricky in both engineering and molecular biophysics is not instrumental noise; it is structural degeneracy. An equilibrium Boltzmann landscape sets a fixed depth ratio between open and closed wells, so its stationary probability vector sums equilibrium weights and forgets every cycle arrow. A steady non-equilibrium pump can hold the exact same fraction in state O while churning around the loop tens of times per second. Only observables sensitive to time ordering—which transition happened first, how tightly current variance shrinks per dissipated joule, or how open dwell distributions bend near time zero—separate those two rooms cleanly.

A direct thought experiment illustrates the distinction cleanly. Film a single gate over one hundred switching events and project the recording forward and in reverse. At thermodynamic equilibrium, the time-reversed film is statistically indistinguishable from the forward film: every loop excursion in one orientation occurs just as often as its mirror opposite, so net cycle flux vanishes by detailed balance. Under external driving, however, running the film backward flips the dominant sequence of steps, turning preferred forward cycles into rare uphill fluctuations. Yet a stationary average such as P_o discards the clock axis altogether, collapsing both movies onto the same single summary fraction.

Closing Movement IV (ES.10–ES.12), this essay turns the lens from gravity scales to the universal thermodynamics of cyclic gates. Building on Lars Onsager's 1931 reciprocity law (DOI 10.1103/physrev.37.405), Udo Seifert's stochastic thermodynamics (DOI 10.1088/0034-4885/75/12/126001), and the thermodynamic uncertainty bounds of Barato and Seifert (DOI 10.1103/physrevlett.114.158101) and Gingrich et al. (DOI 10.1103/physrevlett.116.120601), we test a sharp, falsifiable question: if scalar open probability P_o cannot separate equilibrium from fuel-burning gating, what exact measurements can?

Tactile Analogy · The Three-Station Turnstile

Adding a steady stream around a traffic circle without changing crowd counts

Imagine three subway platforms linked in a triangle where as many riders enter each platform per minute as leave it. If you add four extra clockwise riders per minute on all three tracks at once, each platform still gains four and loses four, so the crowd standing on platform O stays frozen at forty percent. This analogy stops at linear traffic: in real molecules, rates also obey physical barriers and finite substrate concentrations.

Formally, take a 3-state cyclic network C₁ ↔ C₂ ↔ O ↔ C₁ with target stationary occupancies π = (0.35, 0.25, 0.40) and heterogeneous equilibrium bond conductances w₀ = (2.0, 1.5, 1.0) s⁻¹. Consequently, P_o = π₃ = 0.40 stays unchanged for all J ≥ 0.

III · Why Equilibrium Open Bursts Cannot Peak (and Driven Dual-Open Cycles Can)

What if an experiment records only open versus closed dwell times without resolving two closed sub-states? In a 3-state cycle with a single open state O (exit rate e), the open dwell density is exponential with initial slope f'(0) = −e² ≤ 0 even when A = 20 k_B T. Open-time distribution shape cannot detect driving in a 1-open-state graph; only directional transitions between closed states can.

Split O into two coupled open configurations O₁ ↔ O₂ inside a 4-state cycle C₁ ↔ C₂ ↔ O₁ ↔ O₂ ↔ C₁, with exit rates e₁ = 1.0 s⁻¹ (from O₁) and e₂ = 5.0 s⁻¹ (from O₂), and internal rates k₁₂ = k₂₁ = 8.0 s⁻¹ (π_O1 = π_O2 = 0.20, P_o = 0.40). Under detailed balance (A = 0), stationary entry enters O₁ and O₂ in proportion to equilibrium exit flux p_eq = (π_O1 e₁, π_O2 e₂) / (π_O1 e₁ + π_O2 e₂) = (1/6, 5/6). Algebraic cancellation forces f'_eq(0) = −(π_O1 e₁³ + π_O2 e₂³ + π_O1 k₁₂(e₁ − e₂)²) / (π_O1 e₁ + π_O2 e₂) = −42.3333 s⁻² ≤ 0, so f_eq(t) decays monotonically from f_eq(0) = 4.3333 s⁻¹ with t_peak = 0.000 s.

Now break detailed balance so irreversible entry arrives via O₁ (p_drv = (1, 0)) before exiting mainly via O₂. The initial slope flips to f'_drv(0) = k₁₂ e₂ − e₁(e₁ + k₁₂) = +31.0000 s⁻² > 0, climbing from f_drv(0) = 1.0000 s⁻¹ to an interior maximum f_peak = 2.0166 s⁻¹ at t_peak = 0.094 s (94 ms). Notice that A > 0 is necessary but not sufficient for a peak: if direct exit e₁ = 4.0 s⁻¹ exceeds fast forward coupling (k₁₂ = 2.0, e₂ = 3.0 s⁻¹), then k₁₂ e₂ − e₁(e₁ + k₁₂) = −18 < 0 and the burst stays monotone despite non-zero cycle flux.

Why can equilibrium never produce an interior burst maximum on any finite reversible network? Because under detailed balance, the generator inside the open subset O = {O₁, O₂} is symmetrizable by π_O¹/²: its eigenvalues are strictly negative real numbers, and the equilibrium-entry vector weights each relaxation mode with non-negative spectral coefficients. Every completely monotone sum of decaying exponentials drops steepest at t = 0 and bends strictly upward forever after. Turning an open histogram into a peaked clock requires two simultaneous conditions: at least two open states linked inside a loop, and asymmetric driven entry strong enough to beat direct escape from O₁.

From an experimental standpoint, catching that rising phase places a strict requirement on temporal resolution: if a detector bins bursts coarsely beyond roughly 94 ms, the initial upward climb gets averaged together with the exponential tail and disappears. Conversely, resetting a simulation without preserving true equilibrium entry —for instance starting every trajectory artificially at O₁ while asserting A = 0— creates an apparent burst bump even on a reversible network. The interactive observatory and Python harness enforce this separation directly so stationary entry is never conflated with irreversible catalysis.

Physical Unification · Why Order Matters Across 10 Orders of Magnitude

From millijoule check-valves to 20 k_B T ATP cycles, symmetry breaking hides in time ordering

In a macroscopic swing valve, pressure drops maintain a macroscopic one-way flux ratio well above 55× even if the average opening stops at 0.40. In an open chemical network or a nanomachine powered near 20 k_B T, thermal noise occasionally drives backward steps, yet the same directional ratio Φ⁺/Φ⁻ = exp(A/k_B T) measures dissipation exactly.

Across engineered and living systems, the same separation holds. In open chemical reaction networks (Rao & Esposito, 2016, DOI 10.1103/physrevx.6.041064), Harada–Sasa fluctuation-response violations (DOI 10.1103/physrevlett.95.130602), and finite-time speed bounds (Van Vu & Hasegawa, 2021, DOI 10.1103/physrevlett.126.010601), steady-state populations hide cycle currents unless directional asymmetry or phase-resolved responses are sampled. A single scalar occupancy average cannot separate a quiescent catalyst pool resting at detailed balance from a driven catalytic turnover loop consuming substrate free energy; only net cycle affinities summed along fundamental cords break the ambiguity.

In molecular biophysics, human CFTR (ABCC7)—the chloride channel studied in cystic fibrosis research and resolved in ATP-bound phosphorylated Cryo-EM coordinates (Zhang et al., 2018, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1815287115)—exemplifies the driven regime: Csanády, Nairn, and Gadsby (2006, DOI 10.1085/jgp.200609558) showed that ATP binding and irreversible hydrolysis drive an asymmetric gating cycle with peaked open-burst timing, whereas suppressing hydrolysis leaves a symmetric equilibrium pathway whose dwell histogram flattens into monotonic decay even when macroscopic open fraction overlaps. Treating scalar occupancy as a full readout of gating energetics therefore misses the energetic signature that coupled open states make visible in dwell-time shapes and directional transition probabilities.
