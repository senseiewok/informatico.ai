Tensegrity Cable-Strut Domes & Radiative Sky-Cooling Envelopes: Pellegrino Equilibrium Nullspaces, Prestress Stiffening & Zero-Energy Cooling

Synthesizing Folios A.04, A.08, and A.10: an explanatory model—not a structural design method—pairing floating-compression tensegrity frameworks from Snelson, Fuller, Calladine, and Pellegrino with an untested exterior 8–13 μm radiative sky-cooling calculation.

Why it matters

Picture a spoked bicycle wheel. One loose wire spoke bends under two fingers, yet tightening every spoke inside a rigid outer rim lets the wheel carry a rider because downward load slightly relieves existing tension instead of pushing wires into compression. Where the analogy stops: a bicycle wheel relies on a stiff circular rim and predictable hub loads, whereas an architectural cable-strut roof faces wind uplift, rain ponding, cable creep, strut buckling, and support movement that spoke intuition does not check.

Act I — Floating Struts in a Net of Cable, and a Skin That Radiates to the Sky

In the late 1940s, the art student Kenneth Snelson met Buckminster Fuller at Black Mountain College in North Carolina. Snelson built small sculptures in which wooden struts hung apart from one another inside a taut web of cords. Fuller called the principle tensegrity, blending tension and integrity to describe isolated compression bars held inside continuous tension. On paper, pin-jointed frames of that kind can look loose and unstable. At Cambridge in 1978 and 1986, Christopher Calladine and Sergio Pellegrino showed how to count both their internal states of self-stress and their small motions. They also showed when initial cable pull stiffens a frame that would otherwise sway. This folio pairs that structural idea with a separate roof-coating calculation as an explanation, not a design method, and none of its numbers may be used on a real structure.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

In plain words, a long beam spanning an open hall bends under its own weight. Its top edge squeezes while its bottom edge stretches. Thin steel cables cannot resist squeezing at all, yet they carry heavy pull for their weight. In a prestressed cable canopy, short compression struts push outward against a continuous net of taut cables anchored at a perimeter ring. Pulling the cables tight before weather loads arrive helps hold the joint geometry steady in an idealized model. Separately, physics groups have tested bright polymer and multilayer films that reflect sunlight while shedding heat upward through a clear infrared band of the sky. Putting such a film on a cable roof is an untested concept, and passive surface cooling does not replace building ventilation or heatwave safety. This folio is an explanation, not a design method, and none of its numbers may be used on a real structure.

In 1978, Cambridge engineer C. R. Calladine (DOI 10.1016/0020-7683(78)90052-5 [Scite ↗]) generalized James Clerk Maxwell's 1864 frame-counting rule by accounting for both states of self-stress and infinitesimal mechanisms, explaining why tensegrity frames stiffen when cables are pretensioned. Eight years later, Sergio Pellegrino and Calladine (1986, International Journal of Solids and Structures, DOI 10.1016/0020-7683(86)90014-4 [Scite ↗]) formulated matrix subspaces from the equilibrium operator to separate internal cable self-stress from kinematic mechanism modes and to test first-order geometric stiffening. Separately, Raman et al. (2014, DOI 10.1038/nature13883 [Scite ↗]) reported daytime sub-ambient radiative cooling with seven hafnia and silica layers on a silver mirror atop a silicon wafer, and Mandal et al. (2018, DOI 10.1126/science.aat9513 [Scite ↗]) reported porous P(VdF-HFP) polymer coatings under direct sunlight. The phrase 'zero-energy cooling' in this folio's title refers only to passive surface heat emission without a compressor in those material experiments, not to whole-building cooling. Open tools such as compas-dev/compas (github.com/compas-dev/compas) and NREL/EnergyPlus (github.com/NREL/EnergyPlus) are named for context; this folio ran no simulation. This folio is an explanation, not a design method, and none of its untested numbers may be used on a real structure.

Long-span roofs must carry their own dead weight alongside wind uplift, rain ponding, and asymmetric live loads. Tensioned cable-strut roofs carry tensile load paths along steel cables while confining compression to shorter struts, trading bending depth for high perimeter ring forces and anchorage demands. Meanwhile, sunlit roofs absorb solar irradiance and warm up. Combining a prestressed cable-strut canopy with a high-reflectance radiative-cooling skin is an untested research question that mixes two distinct published methods. This folio compares no measured steel weights, designs no real canopy, and claims no air-conditioning or outdoor plaza temperature reduction.

§ I.1 · Classical Obstruction & Structural Boundary

Every enduring equation begins as a human question—an act of patient curiosity by investigators who refused to accept an opaque boundary as the final word. Tracing the intellectual arc of Folio A.12 from its earliest formulation in 1948–1962 (Kenneth Snelson's floating-strut sculptures and R. The 8–13 μm Atmospheric Window Emissivity slider represents how strongly an exposed top coating emits thermal infrared radiation through the clear sky band. Calladine (1978, DOI 10.1016/0020-7683(78)90052-5 [Scite ↗]) extended Maxwell's 1864 bar-and-joint count to three dimensions as b − 3j + 6 = s − m, linking bar count b and joint count j to self-stress count s and infinitesimal mechanism count m. In the matrix formulation of Pellegrino and Calladine (1986, DOI 10.1016/0020-7683(86)90014-4 [Scite ↗]), the equilibrium matrix A maps internal member force vector t to external nodal load vector f through A t = f, and row-echelon reduction extracts its fundamental subspaces: the nullspace ker(A) of dimension s (internal tension states t_0 in equilibrium at zero external load) and the left nullspace ker(Aᵀ) of dimension m (inextensional mechanism displacements u where linear elastic stiffness vanishes, K_E u = 0; later tensegrity software can compute the same bases via singular value decomposition). Adding initial cable prestress t_0 introduces geometric stiffness matrix K_G(t_0); when quadratic form uᵀ K_G(t_0) u > 0 holds for every mechanism mode u, total tangent stiffness K_T = K_E + K_G(t_0) is positive-definite in the idealized model. The readout +42.8 kN/m at t_0 = 185 kN is fixed illustrative text, not the eigenvalue of a solved dome matrix. Live parameter coupling: Cable Prestress Self-Stress Level = 185.0 kN under governing law — in plain words: tensegrity equilibrium balances member forces against loads while prestress adds geometric stiffness, paired with net radiative heat rejection to the sky.

Separately from statics, q_net(T_a) = q_sky_net(T_a) − (1 − R_solar) I_sun mixes published coating data with assumed constants. Citing R_solar = 0.97 from Raman et al. (2014, DOI 10.1038/nature13883 [Scite ↗], seven HfO₂/SiO₂ layers on a silver mirror atop silicon) under assumed I_sun = 1,000 W/m² absorbs 30 W/m². Raman et al. (2014) measured 4.9 °C below ambient and Mandal et al. (2018, DOI 10.1126/science.aat9513 [Scite ↗]) measured about 6 °C on porous P(VdF-HFP) films.
