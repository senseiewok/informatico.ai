Act I — How Thin Masonry Vaults Stand in Compression

In 1864, James Clerk Maxwell published a paper on reciprocal figures and diagrams of forces. He showed that when a drawing of a flat frame is the projection of a closed polyhedron, the bars can carry balanced internal forces. Six years later, Maxwell read that lifted polyhedron as an Airy stress surface for two-dimensional frames. Masonry vaults rely on a similar principle of balance. Stone and clay tile resist squeezing far better than pulling, so a curved vault stands when gravity travels through it in compression. Rafael Guastavino and his son emigrated from Spain in 1881 and built hundreds of thin timbrel tile vaults across the United States. López López and colleagues documented those vaults in 2023. This folio explains how Maxwell's nineteenth-century geometry connects to three-dimensional force polyhedra and a separate roof-cooling calculation. It is an explanation, not a structural design method.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

In plain words, think about how stone and plain concrete respond to force. If you pull a stone block apart, it cracks quickly. If you squeeze that same block, it carries heavy weight. A flat concrete slab bends under gravity, stretching its bottom face and requiring steel bars to resist that tension. An arched vault redirects weight along curved paths so neighboring blocks press together in compression. With modern geometry software, researchers map those internal pushes as three-dimensional polyhedra. When every cell of that force diagram closes, the modeled forces balance at each joint for one assumed load. Even then, perimeter supports or ties must resist the outward sideways thrust. A separate roof calculation also illustrates daytime sky cooling on an exposed vault top. This folio is an explanation of the geometry, not a design method, and none of its numbers may be used on a real structure.

How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: Below, the laboratory lists two teaching parameters before any notation. The Reciprocal Force Polyhedron Scale knob is an overall multiplier on the dual force diagram: scaling it up corresponds conceptually to larger internal forces and horizontal thrust. The Ribbed Vault Crown Rise-to-Span knob represents arch steepness: shallower arches increase outward thrust, while steeper rises direct loads more steeply downward. On this page, dragging either slider updates only its label and visual preview; the four readout cards stay fixed illustrative text and no solver runs.

3D polyhedral graphic statics (Akbarzadeh et al. 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.cad.2015.01.006 [Scite ↗]; tools such as compas-dev/compas) pairs a 3D Form Diagram Γ of bars with a Reciprocal Force Diagram Γ^* of convex polyhedral cells. Each strut e_i is perpendicular to a face f_i^* in Γ^*, with face area A_i^* proportional to axial force N_i (N_i ∝ A_i^*). Because outward area vectors of a closed polyhedron sum to zero, each closed cell encodes nodal equilibrium. Separately, for 2D membrane states (or the horizontal force projection in Thrust Network Analysis, Block & Ochsendorf 2007), an Airy surface Φ(x, y) satisfies σ_xx = Φ_yy, σ_yy = Φ_xx, and σ_xy = −Φ_xy under the tension-positive convention. Negative-definite curvature (Φ_xx < 0, Φ_yy < 0, det(H_Φ) = Φ_xx Φ_yy − Φ_xy² > 0) means the 2D model stress field is compressive everywhere for that load case. The readout det(H_Φ) = +1.84 is fixed illustrative text; no simulation ran here. Live parameter coupling: ζ = 1.15 under governing law — in plain words: a concave Airy stress surface maps second derivatives to compression-only forces, and in three dimensions each bar force scales with the area of its dual polyhedral face.

# 3D Polyhedral Graphic Statics & Airy Stress Surfaces: Maxwell–Rankine Reciprocal Polyhedra, Guastavino Tile Vaulting & Zero-Rebar Floors (Folio A.11)

## The governing relation
`σ_xx = Φ_yy · σ_yy = Φ_xx · Φ_xx, Φ_yy < 0, det(H_Φ) > 0 (2D Airy) · N_i ∝ A_i^* (3D) · q_net(T_a)`

## Rules the essay derives
1. Explanation only, not a structural design method: no thickness, stress, or cooling number from this folio may be used on a real structure.
2. In 3D reciprocal force diagrams Gamma^*, verify each polyhedral cell closes (e.g. ||sum F_i|| < 1e-12 kN) with axial force proportional to face area (N_i proportional to A_i^*).
3. In a 2D Airy model (tension-positive), check Phi_xx < 0, Phi_yy < 0, and det(H_Phi) > 0 for one load case; this does not check buckling, sliding, support movement, or asymmetric loads.
4. For an exposed roof only, distinguish cooling power at air temperature q_net(T_a) = q_sky_net(T_a) - q_solar*(1 - R_solar) from steady-state q_net(T_s) = 0, and estimate Delta T_eq = -q_net(T_a) / h_comb under stated assumptions. Treat 80 W/m^2 and -4.5 C as untested illustrative benchmarks.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (2D Airy Stress Surface): In 2D membrane models (tension-positive), σ_xx = Φ_yy, σ_yy = Φ_xx, σ_xy = −Φ_xy. Maxwell (1864, DOI 10.1080/14786446408643663; 1870) linked plane frames to polyhedra and stress functions; Thrust Network Analysis (Block & Ochsendorf 2007) projects 3D vault forces onto a 2D Airy surface.
- Stage 2 (3D Reciprocal Polyhedra Γ ↔ Γ^*): Separately, 3D polyhedral graphic statics (Akbarzadeh, Van Mele & Block 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.cad.2015.01.006) maps each bar perpendicular to a closed force-cell face, with axial force proportional to face area (N_i ∝ A_i^*); cell closure means nodal forces balance.
- Stage 3 (Concavity & Safety Boundary): Negative-definite Hessian (Φ_xx < 0, Φ_yy < 0, det(H_Φ) > 0) means the 2D model is compressive under one load case. This folio is an explanation, not a design method; none of its untested numbers (including t = 52 mm or det(H_Φ) = +1.84) may be used on a real structure.
- Stage 4 (Separate Exterior Sky-Cooling Pairing): For an exposed roof only (not a floor), assuming a P(VdF-HFP) coating (Mandal et al. 2018, DOI 10.1126/science.aat9513) with R_solar = 0.96 under 890 W/m² absorbs 35.6 W/m². With a lumped illustrative sky exchange of 134.0 W/m² at T_a = 303.15 K and assumed h_comb = 13.9 W/(m²·K), q_net(T_a) = 98.4 W/m² and ΔT_eq ≈ −7.08 °C. Toolbox X.03 (/toolchain/x03/) has a separate 7-voussoir arch STEP AP242 kit.
