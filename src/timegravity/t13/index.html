In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

Why are there long-range electric forces (spin 1) and gravitational forces (spin 2) in nature, yet no long-range spin-3 force—and why can electricity have positive, negative, and neutral particles while gravity pulls every particle in proportion to its energy? Because a massless particle moving at the speed of light has only two transverse polarization directions, while four-dimensional spacetime vectors have four components.

For physics to make the same predictions for every moving observer, the extra longitudinal components must cancel out of every collision. For spin 1, they cancel whenever total electric charge before the collision equals total charge after. For spin 2, the cancellation multiplies each particle's coupling by its momentum vector—and since total momentum is the conserved vector in a collision, the math works if every particle shares the exact same gravitational coupling. For spin 3, it would require conserved squares of momentum, which collisions do not conserve, so the coupling must be zero.
