Quasi-Brittle Concrete Fracture, Bažant Size Effect Law & Hillerborg Process-Zone Scaling

Continuing Folio E.02's concrete placement, E.09's crack healing, and E.11's continuum stiffness: an illustrative fracture-mechanics study coupling Hillerborg's fictitious crack model, Bažant's Type II Size Effect Law, and Bažant–Kazemi regression for initial fracture energy and process-zone length across laboratory beams and deep concrete girders.

Why it matters

Compare tearing a small notched strip of heavy woven cardboard against tearing a wide poster board made of the same card stock. Along the notch tip, loose paper fibers fray and bridge across a finger-width zone about 20 mm wide. On a 30 mm narrow strip, that frayed zone spans nearly the whole remaining ligament, so you feel the strip pull evenly before parting. On a 1,000 mm wide sheet, the same 20 mm frayed zone sits like a tiny pinprick at the notch tip while elastic tension across the broad sheet concentrates energy right into that tip, unzipping the sheet at far lower average stress. This analogy stops where three-dimensional coarse-aggregate interlock and compressive shear friction replace pure tensile fiber tearing.

Act I — Cool Fact & Human Story: Why Small Concrete Beams Test Stronger Than Deep Bridge Girders

In 1976, Swedish structural engineer Arne Hillerborg stood before a puzzling gap in how concrete breaks. A small notched concrete beam cast for a university laboratory bench routinely carried higher nominal stress than a geometrically identical beam poured ten times deeper for a bridge pier or dam spillway. Classical plastic strength rules treated material strength as a fixed number independent of member height, while classical brittle fracture predicted a steep drop in strength as beam depth grew. Neither picture matched real concrete structures. Hillerborg and his colleagues Mikael Modéer and Per-Erik Petersson proposed that concrete ahead of a crack tip does not snap cleanly like window glass. Instead, microcracks and rough stone aggregates share tensile stress across a finite softening zone before splitting apart. Explaining how that process zone sets structural brittleness governs safe scaling from laboratory test benches to full civil spans.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

In plain words, concrete carries a hidden cushion ahead of a growing crack. Before a notch opens all the way, rough stones and tiny microcracks bridge the gap and hold part of the load. In a small bench beam, that bridged zone spans most of the height. The whole cross section shares the pull and fails near its plastic strength. In a deep bridge girder or dam wall, the bridged zone stays about the same size while the girder grows much taller. Now the cushion sits in a thin strip near the notch tip. Elastic energy concentrates right ahead of the crack. Standard small cylinders miss this drop in resistance. As a result, a two-meter girder splits at a lower average stress than a small beam from the same batch, even with matching notch proportions.

In 1984, Zdeněk P. Bažant united those two limits—small-scale plastic strength and large-scale Linear Elastic Fracture Mechanics (LEFM, introduced by Alan Arnold Griffith in 1921, DOI 10.1098/rsta.1921.0006 [Scite ↗]) in a single asymptotic matching law known as the Type II Size Effect Law (Journal of Engineering Mechanics, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(1984)110:4(518) [Scite ↗]). Bažant scaled nominal failure stress using structural depth D, transitional size D_0, and small-scale plastic intercept B f'_t so that small members approach plastic yield while deep members approach square-root fracture scaling. Six years later, Bažant and Mohammad T. Kazemi (1990, DOI 10.1007/BF00047063 [Scite ↗]) showed that plotting inverse squared strength against depth turns the curve into a line whose slope and intercept extract initial Mode I fracture energy G_f and effective fracture process zone (FPZ) length c_f directly from peak loads of notched beams.

Ignoring size scaling when moving from laboratory specimens to deep civil members introduces substantial unconservative error in unreinforced or lightly reinforced shear zones. Under our illustrative calibration (B f'_t = 4.20 MPa, transitional size D_0 = 220.0 mm, effective modulus E' = 34.0 GPa, g(α_0) = 1.12, g'(α_0) = 3.80), a 100 mm laboratory beam carries σ_N = 3.482 MPa (82.9% of B f'_t), a 400 mm beam drops to 2.502 MPa (59.6%), and a 2,000 mm full-scale girder falls to 1.322 MPa (31.5% of B f'_t, brittleness number β_B = 9.091). Extrapolating the small-scale plastic limit 4.20 MPa directly to D = 2,000 mm overestimates nominal fracture capacity by a factor of 3.177× in the model.

Within this state space, any valid proof or physical design must simultaneously satisfy local conservation laws, global boundary conditions, and strict asymptotic scaling—setting the stage for the four-chapter formal derivation in Act II.

Act II — Cross-Field Intuition: Perforated Paper, Aggregate Bridges & the Brittleness Number

Everyday Analogy & Interactive Controls Primer

Everyday Analogy: Compare tearing a small notched strip of heavy woven cardboard against tearing a wide poster board made of the same card stock. Along the notch tip, loose paper fibers fray and bridge across a finger-width zone about 20 mm wide. On a 30 mm narrow strip, that frayed zone spans nearly the whole remaining ligament, so you feel the strip pull evenly before parting. On a 1,000 mm wide sheet, the same 20 mm frayed zone sits like a tiny pinprick at the notch tip while elastic tension across the broad sheet concentrates energy right into that tip, unzipping the sheet at far lower average stress. This analogy stops where three-dimensional coarse-aggregate interlock and compressive shear friction replace pure tensile fiber tearing.

How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: Our interactive fracture laboratory controls two geometric and material scales from the Bažant–Kazemi model. The first slider is Structural Depth (D, from 50 to 2,400 mm), representing the height of a notched concrete beam tested in geometric proportion. Dragging D upward moves the specimen rightward along the size-effect curve, driving brittleness number β_B upward and dropping nominal strength σ_N toward the −1/2 elastic fracture slope. The second slider is Transitional Size (D_0, from 80 to 500 mm), which reflects how large the aggregate-bridged fracture process zone is relative to specimen geometry; raising D_0 expands fracture energy G_f and process-zone length c_f, lifting nominal strength across medium depths.

In Bažant's (1984) energy-release balance, advancing a notch through a quasi-brittle beam releases strain energy from a band of width proportional to aggregate-controlled FPZ length c_f as well as from the outer elastic volume proportional to D. Combining both terms yields σ_N(D) = B f'_t / √(1 + D / D_0), where brittleness number β_B = D / D_0 measures structural size relative to the material-geometry crossover scale D_0. Taking the log–log derivative gives local slope s = d ln σ_N / d ln D = −β_B / [2(1 + β_B)], moving smoothly from 0 in the plastic limit (β_B → 0) to −0.5 in Griffith LEFM (β_B → ∞). Notice the calibration boundary: holding B f'_t = 4.20 MPa, E' = 34.0 GPa, f'_t = 3.20 MPa, g(α_0) = 1.12, and g'(α_0) = 3.80 fixed while varying D_0 scales initial fracture energy G_f = (B f'_t)^2 D_0 g(α_0) / E', process-zone length c_f = D_0 g(α_0) / g'(α_0), and Hillerborg characteristic length l_ch = E' G_f / (f'_t)^2 linearly with D_0. # Quasi-Brittle Concrete Fracture, Bažant Size Effect Law & Hillerborg Process-Zone Scaling (Folio E.15)

A plain-text companion to the essay at https://informatico.ai/engineering/e15/ (`E.15 · Bažant Size Effect & Concrete Fracture · REV 1.0`, `REV 1.0`, redated `2026-11-21`).

## The governing relation
`σ_N(D) = B f'_t / √(1 + D / D_0) · β_B = D / D_0 · G_f = (B f'_t)² D_0 g(α_0) / E' · c_f = D_0 g(α_0) / g'(α_0)`

## Rules the essay derives
1. Enforce strictly positive finite parameters D, D_0, B*f_t, E', f_t, g(alpha_0), and g'(alpha_0) inside geometrically similar notched Type II members (alpha_0 = a_0 / D > 0).
2. Compute nominal strength via Bazant Type II Size Effect Law sigma_N = B*f_t / sqrt(1 + D/D_0) with brittleness number beta_B = D / D_0 and log-log slope s = -beta_B / (2*(1 + beta_B)), avoiding scale-invariant plastic extrapolation.
3. Check unit identity (MPa^2 * mm / GPa = N/m) when evaluating initial Mode I fracture energy G_f = (B*f_t)^2 * D_0 * g(alpha_0) / E', effective FPZ length c_f = D_0 * g(alpha_0) / g'(alpha_0), and Hillerborg characteristic length l_ch = E' * G_f / f_t^2 (mm).
4. Frame Type II size-effect scaling as a Tier T1-T2 structural screening hypothesis that stops at unnotched Type I beams, stirrup-confined or fiber-reinforced shear members, and code design checks.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (From Griffith LEFM to Hillerborg's Fictitious Crack Zone): Griffith (1921, DOI 10.1098/rsta.1921.0006) showed that a sharp crack in a brittle solid grows when stored elastic energy release matches surface energy, scaling nominal strength as D^(−1/2). Concrete is not ideally brittle: ahead of a visible notch, microcracking and aggregate bridging form a finite Fracture Process Zone (FPZ). Hillerborg, Modéer, and Petersson (1976, DOI 10.1016/0008-8846(76)90007-7) modeled this softening zone via tensile strength f'_t, initial fracture energy G_f, and characteristic length l_ch = E' G_f / (f'_t)^2 (424.5 mm at E' = 34.0 GPa, f'_t = 3.20 MPa, G_f = 127.84 N/m).
- Stage 2 (Bažant Type II Size Effect Law & Brittleness Number β_B): For geometrically similar notched members (notch ratio α_0 = a_0 / D > 0), Bažant (1984, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(1984)110:4(518)) matched plastic yield at small D against LEFM at large D via σ_N(D) = B f'_t / √(1 + β_B), with brittleness number β_B = D / D_0. Using illustrative calibration B f'_t = 4.20 MPa and D_0 = 220.0 mm, a 100 mm laboratory beam reaches σ_N = 3.482 MPa (82.9% of B f'_t, β_B = 0.455), while a 400 mm beam drops to 2.502 MPa (59.6%, β_B = 1.818) and log–log slope s = −β_B / [2(1 + β_B)] steepens from −0.156 to −0.323.
- Stage 3 (Bažant–Kazemi Regression for G_f and Process-Zone Length c_f): Bažant and Kazemi (1990, DOI 10.1007/BF00047063) inverted the size-effect curve into a linear relation Y = 1 / σ_N^2 = C D + A, where G_f = (B f'_t)^2 D_0 g(α_0) / E' and effective FPZ length c_f = D_0 g(α_0) / g'(α_0). With LEFM geometry factors g(α_0) = 1.12 and g'(α_0) = 3.80 at D_0 = 220.0 mm, the model yields G_f = 127.84 N/m and c_f = 64.84 mm. Doubling D_0 to 440.0 mm (a tougher aggregate mix at fixed B f'_t) raises G_f to 255.68 N/m, c_f to 129.68 mm, and 400 mm beam nominal strength to 3.040 MPa.
- Stage 4 (Open Hypothesis, Tier T1–T2, and Falsification Condition): Hypothesis (Tier T1–T2): in geometrically similar notched concrete beams without distributed shear reinforcement, nominal failure stress declines along σ_N = B f'_t (1 + D / D_0)^(−1/2) toward Griffith D^(−1/2) scaling as structural depth grows; at D = 2000 mm (D_0 = 220 mm, β_B = 9.091), modeled σ_N falls to 1.322 MPa (31.5%, slope −0.450), whereas ignoring (1 + D / D_0) overpredicts capacity by 3.177× at 4.20 MPa. This Type II hypothesis is falsified in unnotched Type I beams governed by Weibull boundary-layer strength (Bažant & Yu 2009, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(2009)135:2(78)), in heavily stirrup-confined or steel-fiber members that arrest localization, or where aggregate size d_a is comparable to D.

Governing relations (Bažant 1984, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(1984)110:4(518);
Bažant & Kazemi 1990, DOI 10.1007/BF00047063; Hillerborg, Modéer & Petersson 1976,
DOI 10.1016/0008-8846(76)90007-7):
  - Geometrically similar notched Type II concrete members (notch ratio alpha_0 = a_0 / D > 0,
    dimensionless LEFM energy release g(alpha_0) > 0, g'(alpha_0) > 0):
        beta_B = D / D_0                                        (dimensionless brittleness number)
        sigma_N(D) = B * f'_t / sqrt(1 + beta_B)                [MPa]
        s(D) = d ln(sigma_N) / d ln(D) = -beta_B / (2*(1 + beta_B))
  - Initial Mode I fracture energy and effective fracture process zone (FPZ) length:
        G_f = (B * f'_t)^2 * D_0 * g(alpha_0) / E'              [N/m, with MPa^2 * mm / GPa = N/m]
        c_f = D_0 * g(alpha_0) / g'(alpha_0)                    [mm]

Model scope & caveats:
  - Applies strictly to geometrically similar Type II notched structures (alpha_0 > 0) where
    macrocrack localization from a pre-existing notch or stress concentrator governs failure.
  - Varying transitional size D_0 while holding B*f'_t, E', f'_t, g(alpha_0), and g'(alpha_0) fixed

    at constant small-scale plastic strength B*f'_t).

  plastic strength sigma_N = B * f'_t = 4.20 MPa across a full-scale D = 2000 mm girder (where
  the Bažant Type II model gives 1.322 MPa, a 3.177x unsafe overprediction).
"""

def evaluate_size_effect(
    structural_depth_d_mm: float = 400.0,
    transitional_size_d0_mm: float = 220.0,
    small_scale_strength_bft_mpa: float = 4.20,
    effective_modulus_ep_gpa: float = 34.0,
    tensile_strength_ft_mpa: float = 3.20,
    lefm_g_alpha0: float = 1.12,
    lefm_gp_alpha0: float = 3.80,
    planted_defect: bool = False,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Evaluate Bažant Type II Size Effect Law and Hillerborg fracture parameters."""
    params = (
        structural_depth_d_mm,
        transitional_size_d0_mm,
        small_scale_strength_bft_mpa,
        effective_modulus_ep_gpa,
        tensile_strength_ft_mpa,
        lefm_g_alpha0,
        lefm_gp_alpha0,
    )
    if not all(math.isfinite(v) for v in params):
        raise ValueError("All fracture scaling parameters must be finite numbers")
    if any(v <= 0.0 for v in params):
        raise ValueError("D, D_0, B*f'_t, E', f'_t, g(alpha_0), and g'(alpha_0) must be strictly positive")

    brittleness_beta_b = structural_depth_d_mm / transitional_size_d0_mm
    effective_beta = 0.0 if planted_defect else brittleness_beta_b

    retention_ratio = 1.0 / math.sqrt(1.0 + effective_beta)
    nominal_strength_mpa = small_scale_strength_bft_mpa * retention_ratio
    log_log_slope = -effective_beta / (2.0 * (1.0 + effective_beta))

    # Unit identity: (MPa^2 * mm) / GPa = ((1e6 N/m^2)^2 * 1e-3 m) / (1e9 N/m^2) = 1 N/m
    fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m = (
        (small_scale_strength_bft_mpa ** 2) * transitional_size_d0_mm * lefm_g_alpha0
    ) / effective_modulus_ep_gpa
    fpz_length_cf_mm = transitional_size_d0_mm * lefm_g_alpha0 / lefm_gp_alpha0
    # Unit identity: (GPa * N/m) / MPa^2 = 1e-3 m = 1 mm
    hillerborg_lch_mm = (effective_modulus_ep_gpa * fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m) / (
        tensile_strength_ft_mpa ** 2
    )

    if effective_beta <= 0.15:
        regime = "QUASI-PLASTIC LIMIT (β_B <= 0.15, model)"
    elif effective_beta <= 10.0:
        regime = "QUASI-BRITTLE TRANSITION (0.15 < β_B <= 10, model)"
    else:
        regime = "LEFM BRITTLE ASYMPTOTE (β_B > 10, model)"

    return {
        "folio": "E.15",
        "structural_depth_d_mm": round(structural_depth_d_mm, 1),
        "transitional_size_d0_mm": round(transitional_size_d0_mm, 1),
        "brittleness_beta_b": round(effective_beta, 3),
        "nominal_strength_mpa": round(nominal_strength_mpa, 3),
        "retention_pct": round(retention_ratio * 100.0, 1),
        "log_log_slope": round(log_log_slope, 3),
        "fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m": round(fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m, 2),
        "fpz_length_cf_mm": round(fpz_length_cf_mm, 2),
        "hillerborg_lch_mm": round(hillerborg_lch_mm, 1),
        "regime": regime,
        "exact_nominal_strength_mpa": nominal_strength_mpa,
        "exact_log_log_slope": log_log_slope,
    }


def linear_regression_bazant_kazemi(
    depths_mm: list[float],
    strengths_mpa: list[float],
) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Recover B*f'_t and D_0 via Y = 1/sigma_N^2 = C*D + A (Bažant & Kazemi 1990)."""
    n = len(depths_mm)
    if n < 2 or n != len(strengths_mpa):
        raise ValueError("Need >= 2 paired (D, sigma_N) measurements")
    if not all(math.isfinite(d) and d > 0.0 for d in depths_mm):
        raise ValueError("All structural depths D must be finite and strictly positive")
    if not all(math.isfinite(s) and s > 0.0 for s in strengths_mpa):
        raise ValueError("All nominal strengths sigma_N must be finite and strictly positive")
    xs = depths_mm
    ys = [1.0 / (s * s) for s in strengths_mpa]
    mean_x = sum(xs) / n
    mean_y = sum(ys) / n
    sxx = sum((x - mean_x) ** 2 for x in xs)
    if not math.isfinite(sxx) or sxx <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError("Need >= 2 distinct structural depths D for regression")
    sxy = sum((x - mean_x) * (y - mean_y) for x, y in zip(xs, ys))
    slope_c = sxy / sxx
    intercept_a = mean_y - slope_c * mean_x
    if not (math.isfinite(slope_c) and math.isfinite(intercept_a)) or slope_c <= 0.0 or intercept_a <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError("Regression slope C and intercept A must be finite and strictly positive")
    bft_recovered = 1.0 / math.sqrt(intercept_a)
    d0_recovered = intercept_a / slope_c
    return bft_recovered, d0_recovered


def run_checks(planted_defect: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    default_beam = evaluate_size_effect(
        structural_depth_d_mm=400.0,
        transitional_size_d0_mm=220.0,
        planted_defect=planted_defect,
    )
    small_lab_beam = evaluate_size_effect(
        structural_depth_d_mm=100.0,
        transitional_size_d0_mm=220.0,
        planted_defect=planted_defect,
    )
    full_scale_girder = evaluate_size_effect(
        structural_depth_d_mm=2000.0,
        transitional_size_d0_mm=220.0,
        planted_defect=planted_defect,
    )
    tough_beam = evaluate_size_effect(
        structural_depth_d_mm=400.0,
        transitional_size_d0_mm=440.0,
        planted_defect=planted_defect,
    )

    sample_depths = [100.0, 200.0, 400.0, 800.0, 1600.0]
    sample_strengths = [
        evaluate_size_effect(
            structural_depth_d_mm=d,
            transitional_size_d0_mm=220.0,
            planted_defect=planted_defect,
        )["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"]
        for d in sample_depths
    ]

    failures: list[str] = []
    if not (small_lab_beam["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"] > default_beam["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"] > full_scale_girder["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"]):
        failures.append("Nominal strength must strictly decrease as structural depth D grows from 100 mm to 2000 mm")
    if full_scale_girder["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"] >= 1.50:
        failures.append(
            f"Full-scale D=2000 mm girder strength {full_scale_girder['nominal_strength_mpa']} MPa exceeds Type II size-effect bound (<1.50 MPa)"
        )
    if full_scale_girder["exact_log_log_slope"] > -0.40:
        failures.append(
            f"Full-scale D=2000 mm log-log slope {full_scale_girder['log_log_slope']} failed to approach LEFM -0.5 asymptote"
        )
    if abs(default_beam["fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m"] - 127.84) > 0.05:
        failures.append(f"Unexpected G_f at D_0=220 mm: {default_beam['fracture_energy_gf_n_per_m']}")
    if abs(default_beam["fpz_length_cf_mm"] - 64.84) > 0.05:
        failures.append(f"Unexpected c_f at D_0=220 mm: {default_beam['fpz_length_cf_mm']}")
    if abs(default_beam["hillerborg_lch_mm"] - 424.5) > 0.5:
        failures.append(f"Unexpected l_ch at D_0=220 mm: {default_beam['hillerborg_lch_mm']}")
    if tough_beam["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"] <= default_beam["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"]:
        failures.append("Doubling D_0 (tougher aggregate/FPZ) must raise nominal strength at fixed D = 400 mm")

    try:
        bft_rec, d0_rec = linear_regression_bazant_kazemi(sample_depths, sample_strengths)
        if abs(bft_rec - 4.20) > 1e-6 or abs(d0_rec - 220.0) > 1e-6:
            failures.append(f"Bažant-Kazemi regression mismatch: B*f'_t={bft_rec}, D_0={d0_rec}")
    except ValueError as exc:
        failures.append(f"Bažant-Kazemi regression failed: {exc}")

    # Negative domain checks: non-positive depth, duplicate depths, and non-positive/NaN strength must raise ValueError
    rejected_invalid = False
    try:
        evaluate_size_effect(structural_depth_d_mm=0.0)
    except ValueError:
        rejected_invalid = True
    if not rejected_invalid:
        failures.append("Domain check failed to reject D = 0 mm")

    for bad_label, bad_args in (
        ("duplicate D", ([400.0, 400.0], [2.5, 2.5])),
        ("zero strength", ([100.0, 400.0], [0.0, 2.5])),
        ("negative strength", ([100.0, 400.0], [-3.4, 2.5])),
        ("NaN strength", ([100.0, 400.0], [float("nan"), 2.5])),
    ):
        try:
            linear_regression_bazant_kazemi(*bad_args)
            failures.append(f"Regression failed to reject {bad_label}")
        except ValueError:
            pass

    status = "FAIL" if failures else "PASS"
    return {
        "status": status,
        "folio": "E.15",
        "planted_defect": planted_defect,
        "default_beam_400mm": default_beam,
        "small_lab_beam_100mm": small_lab_beam,
        "full_scale_girder_2000mm": full_scale_girder,
        "tough_beam_d0_440mm": tough_beam,
        "overprediction_ratio_at_2000mm": round(4.20 / full_scale_girder["exact_nominal_strength_mpa"], 3),
        "failures": failures,
    }
