Act I — Karl Terzaghi's Effective Stress Principle and Continuous Flight Auger (CFA) Deep Piling

In 1925 in Vienna, the Austrian civil engineer Karl Terzaghi published Erdbaumechanik, setting out a two-phase physical description of waterlogged ground. Builders had long watched heavy masonry structures on soft clay settle unevenly for years. Terzaghi separated saturated soil into solid mineral grains and pore water. Water barely compresses under ordinary building loads, whereas wet clay pores drain slowly. Under his principle of effective stress, the mineral skeleton carries only total overburden pressure minus water pressure inside the pores. Under one-dimensional consolidation, a sudden building load first pressurizes trapped water and shifts to the grains only as water drains. Decades later, foundation crews began drilling hollow-stem augers through compressible clay into firmer strata and pumping cement grout as the auger withdrew. Everything here is an explanatory model, not a geotechnical or structural design method, and no figure on this page belongs on a real structure.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

Imagine setting a heavy stone block onto soft river mud whose tiny spaces are brimming with groundwater. Water cannot compress out of the way in an instant. At first, trapped groundwater carries the added weight while microscopic clay passages slow its escape toward sandy layers. Over months or years, water seeps out and solid grains press closer together, lowering the ground surface. Earthquake shaking in loose wet sand is a separate hazard in which rapid vibration raises water pressure between grains. Where soft clay is thick, Continuous Flight Auger (CFA) piles bore down to denser layers and fill the shaft with pumped cement grout. Deep piles and vertical drains remain site-dependent tools rather than guarantees against settlement or earthquakes. This page offers a simplified classroom explanation rather than a foundation design method, so none of its numbers may be used on real work.

Under Terzaghi's consolidation theory, an undrained surcharge on saturated, low-permeability clay transfers first into excess pore-water pressure before diffusing toward permeable boundaries (Terzaghi 1925). Primary consolidation settlement develops as drainage transfers vertical stress onto the mineral skeleton (Terzaghi, Peck & Mesri 1996). Where compressible clay overlies denser strata, Continuous Flight Auger (CFA) rigs drill a hollow-stem auger to depth and pump cement grout during withdrawal (Brown et al. 2007). Instrumented load tests in sand document how shaft resistance develops along augercast piles (Gavin, Cadogan & Casey 2009, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0000073 [Scite ↗]). Citation tallies for cited papers were checked in Scite (Nicholson et al. 2021, DOI 10.1162/qss_a_00146 [Scite ↗]). Prefabricated vertical drains can shorten clay drainage paths where site testing supports them, yet this untested folio is an explanation, not a foundation design method.

Ignoring slow pore-water drainage on compressible clay leads to long-term consolidation settlement (Terzaghi, Peck & Mesri 1996). Differential settlement and angular distortion can crack walls and frames (Boscardin & Cording 1989, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9410(1989)115:1(1) [Scite ↗]). Cyclic earthquake shearing in loose wet sand raises pore pressure through a separate mechanism needing site-specific liquefaction analysis (Seed & Idriss 1971, DOI 10.1061/JSFEAQ.0001662 [Scite ↗]). Steady surcharge consolidation on this page does not model seismic liquefaction. In practice, engineers combine borings, lab testing, ground improvement, and piles checked for downdrag and lost lateral support; deep piles are one option, not a guarantee.

How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: Two sliders drive the closed-form algebraic model in Act III under fixed illustrative soil and pile assumptions. Foundation Surcharge Load sets the assumed uniform surface pressure from 40 to 260 kilopascals, raising both model settlement and residual water pressure. Consolidation Coefficient sets the assumed pore-water diffusion rate from 0.5 to 8.0 square meters per year across a fixed six-meter drainage path. Higher diffusion values drain excess water sooner at the fixed 3.6-year snapshot. Lower diffusion values combined with heavy surcharge leave higher residual water pressure, triggering the page's illustrative factor-of-safety alert.

With assumed H_dr = 6.0 m and t = 3.6 yr, the time factor T_v = c_v t / H_dr² governs average consolidation (Terzaghi 1925). The browser model uses U_avg = sqrt(4 T_v / pi) for T_v <= 0.286 and U_avg = 1 − (8 / pi²) exp(−pi² T_v / 4) above 0.286, clamped to [0.05, 0.995]. At defaults Delta_q = 120 kPa and c_v = 3.2 m²/yr, T_v = 0.320 gives U_avg = 63.2%. Assumed settlement follows S_c = (Delta_q / 120) × 66.7 × U_avg = 42.2 mm. Residual pressure Delta_u = Delta_q (1 − U_avg) = 44.2 kPa over assumed sigma'_v0 = 200 kPa yields r_u = 0.22 and sigma'_v = 156 kPa. The illustrative CFA formula scales 2,380 kN shaft and 1,980 kN toe terms to Q_ult = 3,865 kN against P_design = 1,550 kN (FS = 2.49). Readers could explore Biot poroelasticity (Biot 1941, DOI 10.1063/1.1712886 [Scite ↗]) in FEniCS/dolfinx (github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx; Scroggs et al. 2022, DOI 10.1145/3524456 [Scite ↗]); none was run here. Live parameter coupling: Delta_q = 120 kPa under governing law — in plain words: excess pore-water pressure diffuses out of soil over time, while total pile capacity sums shaft friction and tip bearing relative to effective vertical stress.

Terzaghi's linear diffusion equation treats the consolidation coefficient c_v as constant, whereas real soft clays lose void space and permeability as compression proceeds (Terzaghi, Peck & Mesri 1996). Variable compressibility and radial drainage around piles alter settlement rates in layered ground. Along real bored and augercast piles, shaft friction mobilizes at small relative slip whereas full base resistance requires larger downward movement (Brown et al. 2007; Gavin, Cadogan & Casey 2009, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0000073 [Scite ↗]). This folio's algebraic formula omits slip compatibility, layered soils, negative skin friction, and cyclic earthquake pore-pressure generation (Seed & Idriss 1971, DOI 10.1061/JSFEAQ.0001662 [Scite ↗]).
