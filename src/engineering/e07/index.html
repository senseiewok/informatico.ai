Act I — From Tacoma Narrows to Multi-Kilometer Suspension Spans & Physics-Informed Bridge Digital Twins

On November 7, 1940, four months after it opened, the first Tacoma Narrows Bridge twisted apart in a wind of about 19 meters per second, or 42 miles per hour. Newspaper editor Leonard Coatsworth left his car on the pitching span and got off on foot before the roadway broke up and fell into Puget Sound. For roughly an hour, the deck had gone into a torsional oscillation at about 0.2 cycles per second, one edge of the roadway rising as the other fell. Film of that collapse has been shown in physics classrooms ever since, where many textbooks blamed simple resonance. In 1971, Robert Scanlan and John Tomko published measured flutter derivatives for bridge decks. In 1991, Yusuf Billah and Scanlan set out why resonance does not fit: self-excited aeroelastic flutter made the moving deck feed its own motion.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

A long suspension bridge acts less like a static road and more like a tethered wing above water. Everyday gusts push and twist the suspended steel deck, and structural damping normally settles that motion like a stiff diving board. Above a critical wind speed, that margin may vanish. Swirling air starts pushing in rhythm with the natural twist of the deck, adding energy on every cycle. Oscillations can grow until girders or hangers fail. Modern long-span crossings streamline the deck or open a central slot so wind passes with less feedback. Engineers also seek ways to estimate weld fatigue between sparse sensors along multi-kilometer spans. This folio pairs bridge flutter mechanics with an untested design study of physics-guided machine learning; no bridge was instrumented and no neural network was trained for this page. This page explains the idea; it is not a design or assessment method, and none of its numbers apply to a real bridge.

Scanlan and Tomko (1971, DOI 10.1061/JMCEA3.0001526 [Scite ↗]) measured flutter derivatives on wind-tunnel deck models, and Billah and Scanlan explained why resonance failed (1991, DOI 10.1119/1.16590 [Scite ↗]): self-excited torsional flutter drove the span. Some long crossings use streamlined box girders, or twin boxes separated by a central slot (Yang et al. 2015, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)BE.1943-5592.0000645 [Scite ↗]). Monitoring buffeting and fatigue across a kilometer or more hits a practical limit because optical strain gauges and accelerometers sit at discrete stations (Chan et al. 2006, DOI 10.1016/j.engstruct.2005.09.018 [Scite ↗]). In 2019, Raissi, Perdikaris, and Karniadakis introduced Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs, DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2018.10.045 [Scite ↗]), adding equation residuals to network loss; later work tested PINNs on plate monitoring (Al-Adly & Kripakaran 2024, DOI 10.1017/dce.2024.4 [Scite ↗]). Whether sparse gauges could estimate weld-toe stress on a real bridge is not yet verified. CSI MasterFormat 05 12 00 and AASHTO LRFD name the subject scope only.

Quasi-static wind rules treat gusts as steady horizontal drag, ignoring how a flexing girder alters its own wake. Under strong wind, a deck may enter torsional or coupled flutter (Jain, Jones & Scanlan 1996, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1996)122:7(716) [Scite ↗]). Below flutter onset, storm buffeting adds spanwise stress cycles, while wheel loads drive local root and toe fatigue cracks in welded rib-to-deck joints of orthotropic steel decks (Ya, Yamada & Ishikawa 2011, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)BE.1943-5592.0000181 [Scite ↗]). Replacing hangers or cracked deck plates on a working crossing is costly. Pairing wind-tunnel derivatives with physics-guided models might help focus inspection earlier, under stated assumptions; that remains an untested research aim.

§ I.1 · Classical Obstruction & Structural Boundary

Every enduring equation begins as a human question—an act of patient curiosity by investigators who refused to accept an opaque boundary as the final word. What appeared for decades to be an intractable computational or experimental pathology was, in reality, a structural phase boundary embedded in the operator domain itself.

Everyday Analogy & Interactive Controls Primer

Everyday Analogy: Hold your palm flat out a car window at highway speed. How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: Our interactive plate has two sliders, and we separate what the browser computes from fixed reference cards. Mean Wind Velocity sets wind speed from 20 to 85 meters per second on a 36-meter model deck (about 6.15 reduced velocity at default wind). Dragging it updates Reduced Wind Velocity and switches Bridge Aeroelastic Verdict at 76.4 meters per second, while Net Torsional Damping stays at its illustrative +2.84 percent reference. PINN Physics Loss Weight adjusts the equation weight from 0.10 to 2.00; moving it updates the slider label and shared stage-overlay vectors, while PINN Virtual Stress Error stays at its untested 1.42 percent reference because no neural simulation runs here.

A deck of width B in wind U oscillates in heave h(x,t) and torsion α(x,t). Scanlan and Tomko (1971, DOI 10.1061/JMCEA3.0001526 [Scite ↗]) linearized lift L_aero (scaled by (1/2) ρ U² (2 B), N/m) and pitching moment M_ae (scaled by (1/2) ρ U² (2 B²), N) via flutter derivatives H_i*(K) and A_i*(K) at K = B ω / U. Sarkar, Jones, and Scanlan (1994, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(1994)120:8(1718) [Scite ↗]) and Jain, Jones, and Scanlan (1996, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1996)122:7(716) [Scite ↗]) used eight and eighteen derivatives with (1/2) ρ U² B for lift or drag and (1/2) ρ U² B² for moment, where A_2*^(1996) = 2 A_2*^(1971) shifts the torsional denominator from 2 I_α to 4 I_α. With per-span inertia I_α (kg·m²/m, making ρ B⁴ / I_α dimensionless), uncoupled 1971 torsional damping is ζ_net = ζ_s − (ρ B⁴ / [2 I_α]) A_2*(K). At U = 52.0 m/s, B = 36 m, and f_α = 0.235 Hz, U_red = 6.15 (K = 1.02); +2.84% and U_crit = 76.4 m/s are fixed illustrative values, not computed from this law and not wind-tunnel data. A coupled PINN loss L_total = L_data + λ_pde ‖R_pde‖² would non-dimensionalize bending E I ∂⁴h/∂x⁴ − H_w ∂²h/∂x² + m ∂²h/∂t² − L_aero(x,t) and torsion I_α ∂²α/∂t² − G J ∂²α/∂x² − M_ae(x,t); 1.42% is an untested reference. Live parameter coupling: Mean Wind Velocity = 52.0 m/s under governing law — in plain words: net bridge damping equals structural damping minus aerodynamic flutter excitation, while the neural surrogate combines measurement error with physics residuals.

As a qualitative picture, flow separating at the leading edge of a bluff girder can act in phase with pitch velocity; when A_2*(K) turns positive, the wind injects torsional energy (Billah & Scanlan 1991, DOI 10.1119/1.16590 [Scite ↗]). Wind-tunnel tests on twin-box decks suggest a central slot modifies gap venting and surface-pressure phase between windward and leeward boxes, raising critical flutter speed when the slot suits the section (Yang et al. 2015, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)BE.1943-5592.0000645 [Scite ↗]). For sensing, automatic differentiation evaluates fourth-order bending derivatives without grid-difference noise (Raissi et al. 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2018.10.045 [Scite ↗]; Al-Adly & Kripakaran 2024, DOI 10.1017/dce.2024.4 [Scite ↗]). Still, storm gusts apply unmeasured loads L_aero(x,t) and M_ae(x,t), so 8-sensor stress recovery remains ill-posed without wind-load estimation.

Two caveats limit any proposed PINN twin: global beam equations cannot resolve local weld-toe notches without submodeling, and surrogates may miscalibrate in extreme storms outside training data.
