Act I — Cool Fact & Human Story: Why Rock Grains Share the Load When Fluid Is Pumped Underground

In 1925, Karl Terzaghi explained how a saturated clay settles by assuming that every mineral grain and every drop of pore water is completely rigid, so fluid carries all of a sudden load before slowly draining away (Folio E.05). Sixteen years later, in 1941, the Belgian-American applied physicist Maurice Anthony Biot asked what happens in three dimensions when the porous rock skeleton itself is stiff—like deep sandstone—and both the solid mineral grains and the pore fluid compress under stress (Journal of Applied Physics, DOI 10.1063/1.1712886). Biot wrote down a linear three-dimensional theory of consolidation in which fluid pressure and solid elastic strain are two halves of one coupled system. In 1954, Alec Skempton measured how much undrained pore pressure jumps when confining stress rises on soils and rocks (Géotechnique, DOI 10.1680/geot.1954.4.4.143), and in 1957 Biot and Willis identified the measurable elastic coefficients linking bulk rock stiffness K_d to solid grain stiffness K_s (Journal of Applied Mechanics, DOI 10.1115/1.4011606). Today, that century-old mathematical framework sits at the center of one of civil and geo-environmental engineering's sharpest debates: can compressed carbon dioxide be injected two kilometers underground for decades without waking dormant faults?

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

In plain words, deep underground rock is not a hollow tank; it is more like a hard, wet sandstone sponge pressed down by two kilometers of rock above it. When engineers pump compressed carbon dioxide or wastewater into the microscopic pores of that sponge, the rising fluid pressure pushes outward against the grains and slightly pries apart any crack or fault cutting through the rock. At the same time, the weight of the rock still presses those fault faces together through friction. If the rock is soft enough and the pressure is kept gentle, friction holds and the fault stays quiet. If the pressure reaches a nearby brittle fault that is already close to its breaking point, the drop in clamping force can let the fault slip and produce an earthquake. This folio walks through the exact balance between rock compressibility, fluid compressibility, and fault friction, using an illustrative closed-form model rather than a site measurement.

Two decades after Biot and Willis (1957), James R. Rice and Michael P. Cleary reformulated linear poroelasticity in terms of drained and undrained Poisson ratios, fluid bulk modulus K_f, and Skempton's pore-pressure coefficient B (Reviews of Geophysics, 1976, DOI DOI 10.1029/RG014i002p00227 [Scite ↗]). Their formulation clarifies why the phase of the pore fluid itself changes the mechanical coupling: in a sandstone with drained bulk modulus K_d = 12.0 GPa, solid quartz grain modulus K_s = 36.0 GPa, and porosity φ = 0.20, the Biot–Willis coefficient is α = 1 − K_d / K_s = 0.667. When the pores hold saline brine (K_f = 2.2 GPa), Skempton's coefficient is B = 0.394, meaning an undrained increase in mean compressive stress Δσ_mean raises pore pressure by Δp_u = +0.394 Δσ_mean. When compressible supercritical carbon dioxide (K_f = 0.20 GPa) displaces brine in the model, B drops to 0.053. Meanwhile, along an open or damaged fault cutting the reservoir or underlying crystalline rock, conservative Mohr–Coulomb stability screening sets the fault effective-stress coefficient to the Terzaghi limit α_fault = 1.0, distinct from the intact rock-matrix value α = 0.667.

The stakes of poroelastic fault mechanics span both industrial decarbonization and seismic hazard. In 2012, Mark D. Zoback and Steven M. Gorelick argued in PNAS (DOI DOI 10.1073/pnas.1202473109 [Scite ↗]) that large-volume CO2 injection into brittle continental interiors carries a high likelihood of triggering small-to-moderate earthquakes that could breach overlying caprock seals. In 2013, William L. Ellsworth documented in Science (DOI DOI 10.1126/science.1225942 [Scite ↗]) how deep wastewater disposal in Oklahoma and other midcontinent basins elevated pore pressure along critically stressed crystalline basement faults, shifting regional earthquake rates. In 2015, Víctor Vilarrasa and Jesús Carrera countered in PNAS (DOI DOI 10.1073/pnas.1413284112 [Scite ↗]; reply DOI DOI 10.1073/pnas.1511302112 [Scite ↗]) that soft sedimentary aquifers have higher stress ratios k_0 and lower stiffness than crystalline basement, while low CO2 viscosity and brine migration restrain pressure buildup near the wellbore. Separating where those two regimes meet—in a quantitative, falsifiable screening model—is the goal of this folio.

§ I.1 · Classical Obstruction & Structural Boundary

Act II — Cross-Field Intuition: Sandstone Sponges, Fluid Cushions & Fault Clamping

Everyday Analogy & Interactive Controls Primer

Everyday Analogy: Picture pressing down on two sponges locked inside a vise—one stiff ceramic-foam block filled with water and one filled with carbonated soda gas—while a slanted wooden joint inside the block is held shut only by friction. When you pump extra pressure into the pores, the fluid pushes outward on the slanted joint. If the vise squeezes tightly from the sides (high horizontal stress ratio k_0), the joint resists sliding even under several megapascals of injection pressure; if the side vise is slack (low k_0 in critically stressed basement), a tiny pressure rise lets the joint slip.

How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: Our interactive geomechanics laboratory exposes two primary parameters from the screening model. The first slider is Injection Reservoir Overpressure (Δp_inj, from 0.0 to 20.0 MPa), which adds fluid pressure above the ambient 20.0 MPa hydrostatic baseline at roughly 2 km depth. Raising Δp_inj lowers the effective normal stress σ'_n clamping a 60°-dipping cohesionless fault and reduces the tensile hydraulic fracture margin against the minimum principal stress min(σ_v, σ_h). The second slider is the Horizontal-to-Vertical Stress Ratio (k_0 = σ_h / σ_v, from 0.56 to 1.20), which sets how tightly the crust squeezes horizontally relative to the 50.0 MPa vertical overburden. Dragging k_0 down toward 0.60 models a critically stressed basement fault where critical overpressure Δp_crit collapses from 9.40 MPa to 0.57 MPa; dragging k_0 above 1.0 enters a reverse-faulting regime where vertical overburden σ_v becomes the minimum principal stress.

In Biot's isotropic 3D poroelasticity, the intact sandstone matrix shares hydrostatic stress between mineral grains and pore fluid via α = 1 − K_d / K_s = 1 − 12/36 = 0.667. Under undrained loading (Δp_u = +B Δσ_mean, compression-positive), Rice & Cleary (1976) express Skempton's coefficient as B = (1/K_d − 1/K_s) / [1/K_d − 1/K_s + φ(1/K_f − 1/K_s)], giving B = 0.394 for brine (K_f = 2.2 GPa) and B = 0.053 for supercritical CO2 (K_f = 0.20 GPa) at φ = 0.20. On a cohesionless fault dipping 60° (fault-plane angle β = 30° from vertical σ_v = 50.0 MPa, p_0 = 20.0 MPa, friction μ_f = 0.60), resolving the 2D stress tensor with uncoupled total horizontal stress σ_h = k_0 σ_v gives σ_n = ½(σ_v + σ_h) − ½(σ_v − σ_h)cos(2β) and |τ| = |½(σ_v − σ_h)sin(2β)|. # Biot 3D Poroelasticity, Skempton Undrained Coupling & Mohr–Coulomb Fault Slip Margin in Geologic CO2 Storage (Folio E.13)

A plain-text companion to the essay at https://informatico.ai/engineering/e13/ (`E.13 · Biot Poroelasticity & Fault Slip Margin · REV 1.0`, `REV 1.0`, redated `2026-11-20`).

## The governing relation
`α = 1 − K_d / K_s · Δp_u = +B Δσ_mean · |τ| / (σ_n − α_fault(p_0 + Δp_inj)) < μ_f`

## Rules the essay derives
1. Compute intact-matrix Biot-Willis coefficient alpha = 1 - K_d / K_s (requiring 0 < K_d < K_s) and Skempton undrained coefficient B = (1/K_d - 1/K_s) / [1/K_d - 1/K_s + phi*(1/K_f - 1/K_s)] under compression-positive sign convention delta_p_u = +B * delta_sigma_mean.
2. Keep fault-plane Terzaghi effective stress alpha_fault = 1.0 distinct from matrix alpha, and apply alpha_fault consistently in both sigma_n_eff = sigma_n - alpha_fault*(p_0 + delta_p_inj) and critical_delta_p = (sigma_n - |tau|/mu_f)/alpha_fault - p_0; state explicitly when total stress sigma_h is held uncoupled from eta_poro * delta_p_inj.
3. Check both shear slip tendency |tau| / sigma_n_eff < mu_f and tensile hydraulic margin min(sigma_v, sigma_h) - (p_0 + delta_p_inj) > 0; treat sigma_n_eff <= 0 explicitly as tensile failure with undefined shear slip tendency.
4. Frame injection stability as a site-dependent Tier T1-T2 hypothesis bounded by stress ratio k_0 and basement fault proximity; never extrapolate closed-form screening outputs into site-specific permit claims.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (Biot–Willis 3D Effective Stress, Continuing E.05): Whereas Terzaghi's 1D soil limit treats solid grains as incompressible (α → 1), stiff porous sandstone with drained bulk modulus K_d = 12.0 GPa and solid mineral grain modulus K_s = 36.0 GPa carries part of the hydrostatic stress in its grain skeleton, yielding α = 1 − K_d / K_s = 0.667 (Biot 1941, DOI 10.1063/1.1712886; Biot & Willis 1957, DOI 10.1115/1.4011606).
- Stage 2 (Skempton Undrained Pore-Pressure Response B): Under compression-positive geomechanics (Δp_u = +B Δσ_mean), Skempton (1954, DOI 10.1680/geot.1954.4.4.143) and Rice & Cleary (1976, DOI 10.1029/RG014i002p00227) write B = (1/K_d − 1/K_s) / [1/K_d − 1/K_s + φ(1/K_f − 1/K_s)]. For φ = 0.20 and brine bulk modulus K_f = 2.2 GPa, B = 0.394; replacing brine with compressible supercritical CO2 (K_f = 0.20 GPa) lowers B to 0.053 in the folio model.
- Stage 3 (Mohr–Coulomb Fault Slip Margin & Stress-Regime Contrast): On a cohesionless fault dipping 60° (fault-plane angle β = 30° from vertical σ_v, normal 30° from σ_h; σ_v = 50.0 MPa, hydrostatic p_0 = 20.0 MPa, μ_f = 0.60, α_fault = 1.0, and uncoupled total stress σ_h = k_0 σ_v), a sedimentary stress ratio k_0 = 0.72 gives σ_n = 39.50 MPa, τ = 6.062 MPa, and Δp_crit = 9.40 MPa; at Δp_inj = 4.5 MPa, slip tendency τ/σ'_n = 0.404 stays below 0.60. In a critically stressed crystalline basement (k_0 = 0.60), Δp_crit drops to 0.57 MPa and Δp_inj = 4.5 MPa raises τ/σ'_n to 0.825 (> 0.60).
- Stage 4 (Open Hypothesis, Tier T1–T2, and Falsification Condition): Hypothesis (Tier T1–T2): in stiff porous sedimentary storage reservoirs where stress ratio k_0 keeps Δp_inj < Δp_crit and compressible supercritical CO2 lowers undrained B while keeping overpressure moderate (Vilarrasa & Carrera 2015, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1413284112), fault slip tendency remains below μ_f; this hypothesis is falsified in any site where pore pressure reaches critically stressed basement faults (Zoback & Gorelick 2012, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1202473109; Ellsworth 2013, DOI 10.1126/science.1225942), drives σ'_n ≤ 0, or where coupled poroelastic stress transfer pushes |τ|/σ'_n ≥ μ_f.

      #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Deterministic verifier for Folio E.13: Biot 3D Poroelasticity, Skempton B & Mohr-Coulomb Fault Slip Margin.

Sign convention (compression-positive geomechanics):
  - Biot-Willis intact-matrix effective stress coefficient: alpha = 1 - K_d / K_s
  - Rice-Cleary (1976) Skempton undrained coefficient:
        B = (1/K_d - 1/K_s) / [1/K_d - 1/K_s + phi * (1/K_f - 1/K_s)]
    with undrained pore-pressure increment delta_p_u = +B * delta_sigma_mean.
  - Lateral poroelastic stress-path factor (passive readout; total sigma_h = k_0 * sigma_v is uncoupled):
        eta_poro = alpha * (1 - 2*nu) / (1 - nu)
  - Cohesionless Mohr-Coulomb fault plane (dipping 90 - beta from horizontal; fault-plane angle beta from sigma_v, i.e. normal beta from sigma_h):
        sigma_n = 0.5*(sigma_v + sigma_h) - 0.5*(sigma_v - sigma_h)*cos(2*beta)
        |tau|   = |0.5*(sigma_v - sigma_h)*sin(2*beta)|
        sigma'_n = sigma_n - alpha_fault * (p_0 + delta_p_inj)
        slip_tendency = |tau| / sigma'_n  (when sigma'_n > 0)
        tensile_margin = min(sigma_v, sigma_h) - (p_0 + delta_p_inj)
        critical_delta_p = ((sigma_n - |tau| / mu_f) / alpha_fault) - p_0

Planted-defect mode (`--planted-defect`):
  Substitutes the intact sandstone Biot coefficient `biot_alpha` (0.667) instead of `alpha_fault` (1.0)
  inside the fault-plane effective normal stress and critical overpressure calculations, illustrating
  how confusing matrix alpha with open-fault Terzaghi alpha_fault overestimates fault stability.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import json
import math
import sys
from typing import Any


def evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(
    drained_bulk_kd_gpa: float = 12.0,
    solid_grain_ks_gpa: float = 36.0,
    porosity_phi: float = 0.20,
    fluid_bulk_kf_gpa: float = 2.2,
    drained_poisson_nu: float = 0.25,
    sigma_v_mpa: float = 50.0,
    stress_ratio_k0: float = 0.72,
    hydrostatic_p0_mpa: float = 20.0,
    delta_p_inj_mpa: float = 4.5,
    fault_angle_beta_deg: float = 30.0,
    friction_mu_f: float = 0.60,
    alpha_fault: float = 1.0,
    planted_defect: bool = False,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Compute Biot 3D poroelastic constants, Skempton B, and Mohr-Coulomb fault slip margins."""
    numeric_inputs = (
        drained_bulk_kd_gpa,
        solid_grain_ks_gpa,
        fluid_bulk_kf_gpa,
        porosity_phi,
        drained_poisson_nu,
        sigma_v_mpa,
        stress_ratio_k0,
        hydrostatic_p0_mpa,
        delta_p_inj_mpa,
        fault_angle_beta_deg,
        friction_mu_f,
        alpha_fault,
    )
    if not all(math.isfinite(v) for v in numeric_inputs):
        raise ValueError("All poroelastic and stress parameters must be finite numbers")
    if drained_bulk_kd_gpa <= 0.0 or solid_grain_ks_gpa <= 0.0 or drained_bulk_kd_gpa >= solid_grain_ks_gpa:
        raise ValueError("Require 0 < K_d < K_s (GPa) for physical Biot-Willis alpha in (0, 1)")
    if fluid_bulk_kf_gpa <= 0.0 or fluid_bulk_kf_gpa > solid_grain_ks_gpa:
        raise ValueError("Require 0 < K_f <= K_s (GPa) for physical Skempton B in (0, 1]")
    if not (0.0 < porosity_phi < 1.0):
        raise ValueError("Porosity phi must lie in (0, 1)")
    if not (-1.0 < drained_poisson_nu < 0.5):
        raise ValueError("Drained Poisson ratio nu must lie in (-1.0, 0.5)")
    if sigma_v_mpa <= 0.0 or stress_ratio_k0 <= 0.0 or hydrostatic_p0_mpa < 0.0 or delta_p_inj_mpa < 0.0:
        raise ValueError("Overburden, stress ratio, and pore pressures must be valid")
    if friction_mu_f <= 0.0 or alpha_fault <= 0.0 or alpha_fault > 1.0:
        raise ValueError("Friction mu_f must be positive and alpha_fault in (0, 1]")

    biot_alpha = 1.0 - (drained_bulk_kd_gpa / solid_grain_ks_gpa)
    c_drain = (1.0 / drained_bulk_kd_gpa) - (1.0 / solid_grain_ks_gpa)
    c_fluid = porosity_phi * ((1.0 / fluid_bulk_kf_gpa) - (1.0 / solid_grain_ks_gpa))
    skempton_b = c_drain / (c_drain + c_fluid)
    eta_poro = biot_alpha * (1.0 - 2.0 * drained_poisson_nu) / (1.0 - drained_poisson_nu)

    sigma_h_mpa = stress_ratio_k0 * sigma_v_mpa
    sigma_3_mpa = min(sigma_v_mpa, sigma_h_mpa)
    beta_rad = math.radians(fault_angle_beta_deg)
    sigma_mean_2d = 0.5 * (sigma_v_mpa + sigma_h_mpa)
    sigma_diff_half = 0.5 * (sigma_v_mpa - sigma_h_mpa)

    sigma_n_mpa = sigma_mean_2d - sigma_diff_half * math.cos(2.0 * beta_rad)
    shear_tau_mpa = abs(sigma_diff_half * math.sin(2.0 * beta_rad))

    effective_fault_alpha = biot_alpha if planted_defect else alpha_fault
    total_p_mpa = hydrostatic_p0_mpa + delta_p_inj_mpa
    sigma_n_eff_mpa = sigma_n_mpa - effective_fault_alpha * total_p_mpa
    tensile_margin_mpa = sigma_3_mpa - total_p_mpa
    tensile_safe = sigma_n_eff_mpa > 0.0 and tensile_margin_mpa > 0.0

    critical_delta_p_mpa = ((sigma_n_mpa - (shear_tau_mpa / friction_mu_f)) / effective_fault_alpha) - hydrostatic_p0_mpa
    slip_margin_mpa = critical_delta_p_mpa - delta_p_inj_mpa

    if sigma_n_eff_mpa <= 0.0:
        slip_tendency: float | None = None
        slip_safe = False
    else:
        slip_tendency = shear_tau_mpa / sigma_n_eff_mpa
        slip_safe = slip_tendency < friction_mu_f

    return {
        "folio": "E.13",
        "biot_alpha": round(biot_alpha, 3),
        "skempton_b": round(skempton_b, 3),
        "poroelastic_stress_path_eta": round(eta_poro, 3),
        "sigma_h_mpa": round(sigma_h_mpa, 2),
        "fault_normal_stress_mpa": round(sigma_n_mpa, 2),
        "fault_shear_stress_mpa": round(shear_tau_mpa, 3),
        "fault_effective_normal_stress_mpa": round(sigma_n_eff_mpa, 2),
        "slip_tendency": None if slip_tendency is None else round(slip_tendency, 3),
        "critical_delta_p_mpa": round(critical_delta_p_mpa, 2),
        "slip_margin_mpa": round(slip_margin_mpa, 2),
        "tensile_margin_mpa": round(tensile_margin_mpa, 2),
        "tensile_safe": tensile_safe,
        "slip_safe": slip_safe,
        "poroelastic_fault_pass": tensile_safe and slip_safe,
    }


def _check_bounds_rejections() -> bool:
    invalid_cases = (
        dict(drained_bulk_kd_gpa=40.0, solid_grain_ks_gpa=36.0),
        dict(fluid_bulk_kf_gpa=72.0),
        dict(porosity_phi=1.0),
        dict(drained_poisson_nu=0.5),
        dict(alpha_fault=0.0),
        dict(delta_p_inj_mpa=float("nan")),
    )
    for kw in invalid_cases:
        try:
            evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(**kw)
            return False
        except ValueError:
            pass
    return True


def run_verification(planted_defect: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    nominal = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(planted_defect=planted_defect)
    co2_fluid = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(fluid_bulk_kf_gpa=0.20, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    basement = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(stress_ratio_k0=0.60, delta_p_inj_mpa=4.5, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    pre_inj = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(stress_ratio_k0=0.56, delta_p_inj_mpa=0.0, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    overpressure = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(delta_p_inj_mpa=11.0, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    tensile = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(delta_p_inj_mpa=22.0, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    reverse = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(stress_ratio_k0=1.20, fault_angle_beta_deg=5.0, delta_p_inj_mpa=32.0, planted_defect=planted_defect)
    terzaghi = evaluate_biot_poroelastic_fault_slip(drained_bulk_kd_gpa=1e-4, fluid_bulk_kf_gpa=36.0, planted_defect=planted_defect)

    checks = {
        "nominal_biot_alpha_0_667": nominal["biot_alpha"] == 0.667,
        "nominal_skempton_b_0_394": nominal["skempton_b"] == 0.394,
        "nominal_eta_poro_0_444": nominal["poroelastic_stress_path_eta"] == 0.444,
        "nominal_slip_tendency_0_404": nominal["slip_tendency"] == 0.404,
        "nominal_critical_dp_9_40": nominal["critical_delta_p_mpa"] == 9.40,
        "nominal_margins_pass": nominal["slip_margin_mpa"] == 4.90 and nominal["tensile_margin_mpa"] == 11.50 and nominal["poroelastic_fault_pass"],
        "co2_skempton_b_0_053": co2_fluid["skempton_b"] == 0.053,
        "basement_k0_0_60_slips": (not basement["slip_safe"]) and basement["critical_delta_p_mpa"] == 0.57 and basement["slip_tendency"] == 0.825,
        "pre_injection_k0_0_56_unstable": (not pre_inj["slip_safe"]) and pre_inj["critical_delta_p_mpa"] < 0.0,
        "overpressure_11mpa_slips": (not overpressure["slip_safe"]) and overpressure["slip_margin_mpa"] < 0.0,
        "tensile_22mpa_hydraulic_breach": (not tensile["tensile_safe"]) and tensile["slip_tendency"] is None,
        "reverse_fault_overburden_tensile_breach": (not reverse["tensile_safe"]) and reverse["tensile_margin_mpa"] < 0.0,
        "terzaghi_incompressible_limit": terzaghi["biot_alpha"] > 0.999 and terzaghi["skempton_b"] == 1.0,
        "invalid_bounds_raise_value_error": _check_bounds_rejections(),
    }
    ok = all(checks.values())
    return {
        "status": "PASS" if ok else "FAIL",
        "folio_id": "E.13",
        "planted_defect": planted_defect,
        "nominal": nominal,
        "co2_skempton_b": co2_fluid["skempton_b"],
        "basement_k0_0_60": {
            "slip_tendency": basement["slip_tendency"],
            "critical_delta_p_mpa": basement["critical_delta_p_mpa"],
            "slip_safe": basement["slip_safe"],
        },
        "checks": checks,
    }


def main(argv: list[str] | None = None) -> int:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Verify Folio E.13 Biot 3D Poroelasticity & Fault Slip Margin.")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    raise SystemExit(main())
