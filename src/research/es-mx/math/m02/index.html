Act I — The Human Story & Historical Turning Point (M.02)

In November 1859, a frail thirty-three-year-old German mathematician named Bernhard Riemann submitted a modest eight-page manuscript to the Berlin Academy of Sciences to mark his election as a corresponding member. Titled simply on the number of prime numbers less than a given magnitude, the paper set aside centuries of tedious integer counting and recast the distribution of primes as a problem of pure wave mechanics. Riemann extended the classical zeta function across the mysterious landscape of the complex numbers and proposed a daring conjecture: that every non-trivial frequency where the function vanishes aligns along a razor-thin vertical meridian. Because prime numbers behave like fundamental acoustic notes, Riemann realized that any stray harmonic wandering away from this line would generate wild, disruptive oscillations across arithmetic. With that quiet eight-page revelation, Riemann launched the deepest, most enduring quest in modern mathematical physics.
