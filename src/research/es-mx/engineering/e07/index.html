Act I — From Tacoma Narrows to Multi-Kilometer Suspension Spans & Physics-Informed Bridge Digital Twins

On the morning of November 7, 1940, a 19 m/s gale across Puget Sound locked the 853-meter main span of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge into a 0.20 Hz antisymmetric torsional limit cycle that tore the plate-girder deck apart within an hour. Theodore von Karman initially pointed to Kármán vortex shedding, but three decades later, in 1971, Robert H. Scanlan and John J. Tomko proved that bridge collapse is not passive resonance—it is self-excited aeroelastic flutter governed by eighteen motion-dependent aerodynamic derivatives (H_1*..H_4*, A_1*..A_4*, P_1*..P_6*) where torsional wind moments inject negative aerodynamic damping into the structural deck.

Today, civil structural engineers span straits exceeding 2,000 to 3,300 meters using streamlined twin-box steel girders (CSI 05 12 00, AASHTO LRFD Bridge Design Specifications). Yet monitoring fatigue, hanger cable tension, and flutter margins across a 3-kilometer span under typhoons and heavy freight traffic faces a fundamental observability bottleneck: physical accelerometers and strain gauges can only be placed at sparse nodes. In 2019–2026, Maziar Raissi, Paris Perdikaris, and George Em Karniadakis's Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs, DOI DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2018.10.045 [Scite ↗]) transformed Structural Health Monitoring (SHM): by embedding the coupled Euler–Bernoulli vertical bending and Saint-Venant/Vlasov torsional aeroelastic PDEs directly into the neural loss function L_total = L_data + lambda_pde L_pde, a bridge Digital Twin reconstructs full-field stresses, deflections, and Scanlan flutter margins at every uninstrumented meter of the span.

Act II — How It Actually Works: Scanlan Aeroelastic Flutter + PINN Virtual Sensing

A suspension bridge deck of width B subjected to mean wind velocity U oscillates simultaneously in vertical heave h(x, t) and torsional pitch alpha(x, t) at reduced frequency K = B * omega / U. Scanlan linearized the self-excited aerodynamic lift L_ae and pitching moment M_ae in terms of dimensionless flutter derivatives H_1*(K)..H_4*(K) and A_1*(K)..A_4*(K).
