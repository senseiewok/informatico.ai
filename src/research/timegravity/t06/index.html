Time & Gravity · T.06 · A proposal and the two experiments that bound it · Step 7 of 9 · 13 min read

Does Gravity Collapse the Wave?

One proposal says gravity itself ends a superposition: a mass in two places pays an energy E_G for the difference between its two gravitational fields, and the superposition decays in about ħ/E_G. The same proposal predicts that charged matter should emit a faint spontaneous radiation with no external cause. Two underground detectors looked for that glow and did not see it. This page computes the model, recomputes the bound from the raw counts, and draws the line on the side the data put it.

I · The proposal

Diósi in 1987 and Penrose in 1996 proposed that a quantum superposition of two mass distributions is unstable, with a lifetime of order ħ/E_G, where E_G is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the two distributions; for a uniform sphere of mass M and radius R displaced far from itself, E_G = (6/5) G M²/R. This is a proposal, and the label says so. The instrument evaluates E_G in closed form and by direct numerical integration, which agree to better than one part in a thousand, so that whatever the model's fate, its arithmetic on this page is right.

For a sphere of a hundredth of a nanogram and a micrometre across, displaced by its own diameter, the model's collapse time is a few hundredths of a second. For a molecule of 25,000 atomic mass units treated as a sphere a nanometre across it is tens of thousands of years. Matter-wave interferometry, which is where superpositions are built, is nowhere near the first number yet; the test came from somewhere else.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

If a thing is in two places at once, its gravity is too. The proposal says nature dislikes that and resolves it, faster the heavier the thing. Nobody has caught a superposition collapsing that way. But the same proposal has a side effect: the jitter that keeps collapsing a particle's position would make charged particles radiate, always, a little. That is a prediction you can test in a quiet room with a detector and patience.

Two groups did, deep underground where cosmic rays cannot reach. They counted the photons they expected from ordinary radioactivity, and almost nothing more. The model in its simplest form would have produced thousands of times that. So that form is out, and the page says what is left.

Everyday Analogy & Controls Primer

Everyday analogy: a suspect claims to be in two cities at once. You cannot catch the moment he picks one. But a person who is constantly being yanked between two cities would leave a trail: receipts, footprints, a hum. You search the quietest room in the country for the hum. You find the usual noise and nothing else. The story that he is being yanked, in its plainest version, is no longer tenable.

How the controls work in plain words: the mass and size sliders choose the thing in two places; the model's clock on the left shows how fast it would be forced to choose. The right panel is the hum search: the line is how quiet the room was, and the model's preferred setting sits on the loud side of it. The knockout removes gravity, so the model predicts nothing at all. The planted defect paints the loud side green, which the verifier refuses.

II · The bound, recomputed from the counts

dΓ/dω = (2/3) G e² N² N_a / (π^{3/2} ε₀ c³ R0³ ω) ⇒ z_s(R0) = a / R0³, a ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻²⁹ m³

In a germanium detector at Gran Sasso, 576 photons were counted in the 1,000 to 3,800 keV window over about 62 days against 506 expected from background, and the predicted spontaneous emission, which scales as 1/R0³ with R0 the size of the nuclear mass density, sets R0 > 0.54 × 10⁻¹⁰ m with probability 0.95; the parameter-free version of the model, which fixes R0 at the width of the nuclear wave function in the crystal, 0.05 × 10⁻¹⁰ m, is excluded. The verifier takes the counts and the paper's rate constant, computes a classical 95 % upper limit on the excess (about 111 photons) and inverts z_s = a/R0³: it finds R0 > 0.545 × 10⁻¹⁰ m, within 2 % of the published bound. At the parameter-free value the same formula gives about 1.4 × 10⁵ photons, three orders of magnitude above what was seen.

In 2026 the XENONnT collaboration searched its first science run, 1.16 tonne-years of liquid xenon exposure in the 1 to 140 keV electronic-recoil range, for the same spontaneous radiation and set R0 > 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m at 90 % confidence (4.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m at 95 %), about a factor of five beyond the previous world-leading limit. It is the strongest bound and, inside the ledger's replication window, a SINGLE RESULT; the row will read BOUND once it is two years old or another detector reaches it. The same paper constrains an unrelated collapse model, CSL, by two orders of magnitude; CSL is not a gravity model and is outside this page.

Matter-wave interference has been observed for molecules beyond 25,000 atomic mass units, the heaviest superpositions built so far. In the model, such a molecule treated as a nanometre-sized sphere would take tens of thousands of years to collapse, so interferometry does not yet test the proposal directly; the radiation does, and that is why the bound comes from an underground detector rather than a beam.

Stage 1 · E_G two ways

E_G(d) = 2 U_self − U_int(d) with U_self = (3/5) G M²/R and U_int the interaction energy of two uniform spheres (G M²/d beyond contact, a quintic polynomial in d/2R when they overlap, 2 U_self when they coincide). The verifier integrates the potential of one sphere over the volume of the other on a grid and requires agreement with the closed form within 10⁻³; E_G → (6/5) G M²/R at large d and → 0 at d = 0.

Stage 2 · The predicted radiation

Donadi et al. Eq. 4: dΓ/dω = (2/3) G e² N² N_a / (π^{3/2} ε₀ c³ R0³ ω), with N the atomic number and N_a the number of atoms. Integrated over the 1,000 to 3,800 keV window and 62 days this is z_s = a/R0³ with a ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻²⁹ m³ (the paper's value, which includes the detector's efficiency). The verifier recomputes a without efficiency and gets about 1.0 × 10⁻²⁸ m³, within a factor of ten.

Stage 3 · From counts to a bound

576 counted, 506 expected: a classical 95 % upper limit on a Poisson signal over known background gives about 111 photons. Setting a/R0³ ≤ 111 gives R0 > 0.545 × 10⁻¹⁰ m, against the paper's 0.54 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (the paper uses a Bayesian treatment; the verifier accepts agreement within 20 %).

Stage 4 · The side of the line

Allowed: R0 at or above the bound. Excluded: below it, including the parameter-free 0.05 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (about 1.4 × 10⁵ predicted photons). The planted defect inverts the orientation and would call the parameter-free value allowed; the verifier fails, as it must. XENONnT's 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m lies on the allowed side and tightens it.

III · What this page does not say

The bound applies to the non-dissipative, parameter-free form of the model, the form Penrose proposed. Variants with dissipation, or with R0 left as a free parameter above the bound, are not excluded by these experiments, and this page does not assess them. The page also does not say that superpositions never collapse; it says that gravity, in this one proposed way, does not do it at the rate its parameter-free form predicts.

What a superposition of two gravitational fields is, if it neither collapses at the proposed rate nor has been shown to entangle, is not a question these bounds answer; they close one road and leave the destination open. The next page takes up the strangest version of that question: an equation for the whole with no time in it at all.

IV · Where the smearing may live

The model has one free length, the width σ over which each mass is smeared, and the radiation it predicts has bounded that length from below three times: analysis of LISA Pathfinder data put it above 40 fm in 2017, the Gran Sasso germanium detector above 0.54 Å in 2021, and XENONnT above 4.9 Å in 2026. The ladder draws them as the left edge of the allowed region. Everything else on it is a use of the same parameter, and the uses disagree.

Two very different uses want σ small. The authors of the hybrid form that the 2025 entanglement letter builds on called it 'the natural cutoff σ ∼ 10⁻¹² cm', a hundredth of a picometre. The one biological use of the model, Orch-OR, holds that consciousness-related collapse follows 'the specific Diósi-Penrose scheme'; on Penrose's formula the kernel finds that a single tubulin, 55 kDa, in two places a nucleon radius apart lives 6.5 × 10⁸ s at σ = 10⁻¹⁴ m and about 10³⁷ s at the laboratory rungs near 44 µm, some 10²⁰ ages of the universe: twenty-nine orders of magnitude between the two uses of one number. The page does not assess Orch-OR; it reports what the model's own formula gives at each rung. Its planted defect for this kernel drops the small-displacement series, so the direct difference returns rounding noise six orders of magnitude off and breaks the σ³ law between the top rungs, which the verifier rejects.

At the top of the ladder sits the 2025 letter's 50 to 250 µm, the range in which the model entangles two masses in a laboratory; just below it, at 44 µm, the wedge kernel of T.05 finds the model's softened pull removing as much of the Eöt-Wash torque as the Yukawa that test excludes at 95 %. On this site's estimate the inverse-square tests leave room only below that line, and the entanglement letter needs the range above it. A 2025 preprint on the smearing profiles of these models notes that for this parameter 'upper bounds can be put by observing no deviations from Newton's law of gravitation'; it works none out and names no experiment. The 44 µm rung is this site's own estimate from the Eöt-Wash comparison on T.05, not the preprint's.The same torsion balances enter a second squeeze: in 2023 Oppenheim and co-workers showed that any consistent theory with classical gravity and quantum matter must trade decoherence of the matter against diffusion of the field, bounded that diffusion with Cavendish-type and rotating-attractor measurements at D₂ ≤ 10⁻⁴¹ kg² s m⁻³, and named the Diósi–Penrose kernel as a member of the class.

No single σ serves every use on the ladder: the bounds want it above 5 Å, the biological use needs the bottom of the ladder, the entanglement letter needs the top, and the inverse-square tests, on this site's estimate, leave room only under 44 µm. The ladder shows that and the page draws no conclusion beyond it; which rung gives way is for the experiments to decide.
