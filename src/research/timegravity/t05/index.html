Does Gravity Entangle, and Would That Settle It?

Put two small masses each into two places at once and let them pull on each other through gravity alone. If the pull is carried by something quantum, the pair comes back entangled, by a phase this page recomputes from G, the masses, the distances and the time. Nobody has run the experiment. And since 2025, into 2026, the literature disputes what a positive result would prove. This page draws the three answers at equal weight and labels the whole question OPEN.

I · The proposal, and the phase it predicts

In 2017 two papers proposed the same test: hold two micron-size masses, each in a superposition of two positions, close enough to pull on each other through gravity and far enough that Casimir–Polder forces stay a tenth of the gravitational pull; if the two masses come back entangled, the field that connected them is, on the papers' argument, a quantum entity. The first paper gives a geometry: masses of 10⁻¹⁴ kg, traps 450 µm apart, each mass split by 250 µm, a hold of 2.5 s, a pressure of 10⁻¹⁵ Pa and an internal temperature of 0.15 K. The second gives the general argument for why entanglement would be sufficient evidence. Both are proposals, and both rows are labelled so.

Each of the four branches (left–left, left–right, right–left, right–right) accumulates the Newtonian phase φ = G m² t/(ħ d) for its own separation d; the two-mass state is a product exactly when Δφ = φ_LR + φ_RL − φ_LL − φ_RR is a multiple of 2π, and otherwise its concurrence is |sin(Δφ/2)|. The label is DERIVED: the concurrence formula is Wootters's for a pure two-qubit state, and the potential it is fed is the Newtonian one, the law the parent MEASURED row confirmed between millimetre gold spheres at a surface distance of 400 µm. For the 2017 geometry the kernel finds the RL and LR branch phases to exceed the LL phase by 0.44 rad and −0.13 rad (the paper's order-of-magnitude 0.7 and −0.2), an entanglement phase of 0.31 rad and a concurrence of 0.16; the verifier recomputes the concurrence from the reduced state's purity and requires agreement to one part in 10¹².

A local dephasing rate γ on each mass shrinks the off-diagonal terms by e^{−γt}; the Peres negativity of the resulting mixed state, computed from the partial transpose, equals half the concurrence when γ = 0 and reaches zero for the 2017 geometry at γt = 0.16, so the entanglement survives only while the dephasing phase budget stays below the entanglement phase's own order. The paper's own requirement reads the same way: at 10⁻¹⁵ Pa and 0.15 K it estimates a collisional decoherence time of the order of the 3.5 s the masses spend falling, and a witness value of about 1.16 against a threshold of 1. This page's dephasing model is a single rate, not a laboratory budget; the paper's witness and the kernel's negativity are different measures of the same state.

In Plain Words · Extra-Easy Explanation

Two things are entangled when neither has a state of its own, only the pair does. Gravity is a pull that depends on distance. If each mass is in two places, there are four distances, and each pair of positions collects a slightly different amount of 'gravitational time'. Those four amounts do not add up to something each mass could carry alone; the leftover is the entanglement phase. On this page it is 0.31 radians after 2.5 seconds, and the dial would read 1 after 25 seconds.

The hard part is not the pull, which is there. The hard part is keeping two specks of matter in two places for seconds without a single gas molecule or a single photon noticing where they are. Nobody has done it yet. And a second hard part appeared in 2025: physicists disagree about whether seeing the entanglement would prove gravity is quantum. So the page says OPEN, twice.

Everyday Analogy & Controls Primer

Everyday analogy: two dancers each step left or right, and a rope between them tightens by an amount that depends on how far apart they land. If the rope is a real rope, the four possible tightenings leave a trace that neither dancer could have made alone, and the two are coupled. If a stagehand only watches each dancer and tugs the other by hand according to the average, the dancers feel a pull, but no trace of the pair survives. The third case, a rope nobody agrees is real, is what the 2025 papers argue about.

How the controls work in plain words: the mass slider makes the rope stronger as the square of the mass; the separation slider weakens it; the split slider makes the four distances more different, which is what entangles; the hold-time slider lets the phase grow; the dephasing slider lets the room look. The channel buttons are the knockout: the same rope seen as quantum, as classical LOCC, or as disputed. The planted defect lets the classical LOCC channel copy the quantum curve, which the verifier refuses.

II · Three channels, equal weight

A classical channel can carry the force without carrying the entanglement: in the 2014 classical-channel model gravity acts as a continuous measurement of each mass's position fed back as a force on the other, which reproduces Newton's law on average, adds a minimum decoherence, and, being local operations with classical communication, can create no entanglement at all. That is the second channel of the instrument, drawn at the same stroke weight as the first. The kernel builds it as a mean field: each mass feels the average of the other's two positions, the four phases take the product form α_i + β_j, and the entanglement phase is zero to one part in 10¹² on a grid of twenty-seven geometries. In the 2017 argument this is the case a positive result would exclude.

Whether every classical account of gravity falls into that second channel is disputed: a 2025 paper argues that a classical gravitational interaction can transmit quantum information and generate entanglement through physically local processes when matter is treated by quantum field theory, and a 2025 letter finds that the Diósi–Penrose dynamics with classical gravity entangles two separate particles, so that 'GIE detection is not enough to validate quantum gravity'; two preprints and a 2026 paper reply that classical gravity cannot mediate entanglement by local means and that the perturbative result is precluded by an exact derivation, and a 2025 preprint by the witness's own proposers argues that the collapse-based models which entangle do so through nonlocal features and do not escape the witness. This is the third channel. The kernel gives it no curve of its own, only the interval between the two published answers, no entanglement at all and as much as the quantum channel gives, drawn as a hatched band with neither edge marked as the expected one; the page does not pick a side, and the row labels say why: the papers are young, two are preprints, and none is a measurement.

The band's upper edge can be computed. In the Diósi–Penrose model as the 2025 letter writes it, gravity is classical, matter is quantum, and the mass density is smeared over a width σ, the model's only free parameter and the same R0 that T.06 bounded from below; with the free motion switched off the model integrates in closed form, and the kernel reproduces the letter's result: the model predicts entanglement only when the masses' nearest separation is below 0.8508σ (the letter: 0.8501σ in its text, 0.850872σ in its appendix), that entanglement is transient, and for the letter's example geometry (10⁻¹⁵ kg, split 23 µm, 24 µm apart) nothing is entangled at σ = 50 µm while at σ = 75, 100 and 250 µm the negativity lasts about 5 hours, 16 hours and 20 days. On the same model the smearing softens gravity itself below about 2σ: at σ = 75 µm it puts the pull between those two masses at 24 µm at 18 % of Newton's. For the 2017 geometry, with a split of 250 µm, the model entangles only for σ above 525 µm and shows less than one part in a thousand of negativity at 2.5 s, because its entanglement takes hours to build. The page's planted defect for this kernel drops the model's own decoherence and keeps its phases; the critical separation then disappears, and the verifier rejects it. All of this is one model's arithmetic; the label is PROPOSED.

The kernel can also say where the model's entangling power sits. Its generator has two parts: an imaginary part, a commutator with the smeared pair potential −Gm₁m₂ erf(d/2σ)/d, and a real part, the model's own noise. With the noise switched off and only the potential kept, the evolution is unitary, the four-branch state stays pure, and the negativity is that of the smeared phase, exactly this page's quantum channel, 0.078 for the 2017 geometry, as σ → 0; with the potential switched off and only the noise kept, the negativity is zero wherever the kernel looks, as expected of a dephasing channel in the position basis. In full the model gives less than its potential alone: for the letter's geometry at σ = 75 µm, 0.024 against 0.152 after 10⁴ s, and at σ = 50 µm nothing against 0.363, the noise, stronger at smaller σ, having removed it all. In this construction the entangling term is the commutator with the smeared pair potential, the term left once the classical field is traced out; the noise term only takes away. Whether such a term counts as classical mediation is the dispute itself, and the page states the decomposition and no more.

The band's lower edge is computed from the classical-channel rate: in the 2014 model the measurement-and-feedback channel reproduces the linearised gravitational spring K = 2Gm²/d³ and adds position decoherence at KΔx²/(2ħ) per mass, 'minimal in the sense that, if it were any smaller, evolution ... would immediately entangle'; the kernel finds that the dephasing threshold at which this page's negativity vanishes is exactly that rate in the small-split limit (ratio 1.000 at a 2 µm split, 1.43 at the 2017 split of 250 µm, where the linearisation is 30 % low on the phase). The same threshold, 0.156 over 2.5 s or 0.062 per second, is within 1 % of the 1/τ_G of a 2021 decoherence study of these proposals, 0.0628 per second on its formula with the 2017 numbers, which it rounds to ≃0.06 s⁻¹ and above which it finds the pair separable at all times. So the two edges are computed, not chosen: the lower one is the least noise a classical channel of that kind must add on its own model, the upper one is what one named classical model predicts, and between them the page still picks no side.

The proposed experiment is also a collapse test. If gravity is quantum and the Diósi–Penrose collapse exists besides, with Newton's law untouched, the collapse decoheres the two masses on the same two-body kernel the hybrid model uses for its noise, at a rate that grows with the mass squared and falls as the smearing grows; on this page's kernel the quantum channel's negativity of 0.078 for the 2017 geometry is reduced to zero at 2.5 s unless σ is above 130 µm, and to half unless σ is above 177 µm, while the letter's closer geometry keeps some unless σ is below 14 µm. The threshold is set by the geometry's size and barely by the mass or the hold time while the entangling phase stays small, since both the phases and the collapse damping grow as m²t: scaling the 2017 geometry by a half or by two moves it to 66 and 259 µm, while a tenfold change of mass at fixed geometry leaves it at 130 µm. The 2017 proposal named spontaneous collapse as a mechanism that 'will typically lead to a strong loss of coherence' and gave no number; these are the numbers this page computes on that model. A positive witness at the 2017 geometry would therefore set a lower bound of 130 µm on the collapse length, more than five orders of magnitude above the radiation detectors' 4.9 Å; in that scenario Newton's law is untouched, so the hybrid form's Eöt-Wash estimate does not apply to it. A null result would not by itself separate classical gravity from quantum gravity with collapse. A 2021 study of decoherence in these proposals found that the other collapse model, CSL, would hinder the entanglement for any value of its parameters; it did not treat the Diósi–Penrose case, which is this page's computation.

The two laboratory uses of σ sit on one geometric axis. For any geometry both thresholds scale with the nearest approach d and depend otherwise only on the ratio of split to nearest approach: the letter's hybrid dynamics entangles the pair only for σ above 1.2 to 38 times d as the split grows from a thirtieth of d to thirty-two times it, while quantum gravity with pure collapse keeps a surviving witness for σ above 0.37 to 1.0 times d over the same range. So a positive witness would read as a lower bound on σ in either model, and the two bounds differ by a factor of 3.3 at small splits, 3.8 at the letter's geometry, 4.1 at the 2017 geometry and about 11 at a split eight times the gap; the page computes the two curves and draws no conclusion from the gap between them, since no witness has been measured. At the T.05 estimate of 44 µm the hybrid dynamics would entangle only geometries whose nearest approach is below 36 µm at tiny splits and below 19 µm at equal split and gap, against a Casimir floor of 17 µm for 10⁻¹⁵ kg; the window is about two micrometres wide at equal split and gap, and whether anything sits in it is not determined by this page.

III · How far the laboratories are

Gravity has been measured on the table with milligram-scale masses by two groups: in 2021 between two gold spheres of about 1 mm radius and 90 mg, on a torsion pendulum at 12.7 mHz over 350 hours, down to a surface distance of 400 µm; and in 2024 by a levitated 0.4 mg magnetic particle at 26.7 Hz with a force noise of 0.5 fN/√Hz, sensing kilogram source masses about half a metre away. Both are classical measurements of a classical pull, by different collaborations, which is what MEASURED requires. Neither mass was in two places; the proposal's masses are between seven and ten orders of magnitude lighter still.

    manifest = json.loads((FILES_DIR / "manifest.json").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
    out = {}
    for entry in manifest["files"]:
        raw = (FILES_DIR / entry["filename"]).read_bytes()
        assert len(raw) == entry["bytes"], f"{entry['filename']}: byte count mismatch"
        digest = hashlib.sha256(raw).hexdigest()
        assert digest == entry["sha256"], f"{entry['filename']}: sha256 mismatch"
        out[entry["filename"]] = digest[:12]
    ref = json.loads((FILES_DIR / "t05_reference.json").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
