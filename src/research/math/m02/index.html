Act I — The Human Story & Historical Turning Point (M.02)

In November 1859, Bernhard Riemann submitted a six-page paper to the Berlin Academy titled 'On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude.' In it, he extended Euler's zeta series sum n^(-s) into the complex plane and conjectured that every non-trivial zero of zeta(s) lies on the razor-thin vertical critical line Re(s) = 1/2. Because the primes are the harmonic overtones of these zeros, a single zero straying toward Re(s) = 1 would create wild, unexpected waves in the distribution of prime numbers.

While the full Riemann Hypothesis remains open, 20th-century analytic number theorists—Harald Bohr, Edmund Landau, Albert Ingham (1940),Hugh Montgomery (1971), and Martin Huxley (1972)—discovered something almost as powerful for practical applications: zero-density estimates. Even if hypothetical exceptions N(sigma, T) exist to the right of Re(s) = sigma > 1/2 up to height T, Large Sieve orthogonality and Dirichlet polynomial mollifiers prove they must be exponentially sparse. In 2025–2026, Anthropic's formal-math Zeta23 and Challenge/XiPrime.lean projects translated these classical Complex Analysis contour estimates into machine-checked Lean 4 theorems with zero unverified asymptotics.
