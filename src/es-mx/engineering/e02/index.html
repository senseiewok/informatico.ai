Este monográfico interactivo verifica la ley gobernante τ = τ_0 + K γ̇^n · r_p / R = τ_0 / τ_w · ΔP/L = 2τ_w/R. Por qué importa en la vida real: Al exprimir pasta dental o mortero por un tubo no se deforma todo el volumen: un cilindro sólido central se desliza intacto como un tapón sobre una delgada capa lubricante sometida a corte.

En Palabras Sencillas (Explicación Accesible): En 1922, el químico Eugene Bingham publicó Fluidity and Plasticity, donde explicó que materiales como pinturas y suspensiones de arcilla no fluyen de manera convencional: requieren un esfuerzo mínimo antes de comenzar a moverse. En 1926, Winslow Herschel y Ronald Bulkley publicaron mediciones con soluciones de hule en benceno y generalizaron la idea: una vez en marcha, la resistencia de estos fluidos cambia según la velocidad del movimiento. Para visualizar esto en la vida diaria, piensen en cómo sale la pasta de dientes de su envase: mantiene una forma sólida y firme sobre el cepillo, pero fluye dócilmente cuando ustedes aprietan el tubo con suficiente fuerza. En la construcción de grandes edificios y puentes, el concreto fresco que se bombea por tuberías de acero se comporta de forma parecida. En el modelo de esta página, la mezcla forma un cilindro sólido en el centro; en el concreto real, además, la pasta de cemento forma una capa lubricante junto a la pared del tubo.

Mecanismo Técnico, Fuerzas en Competencia y Umbral Crítico: El bombeo eficiente de concreto de alto desempeño depende del equilibrio entre dos fuerzas fundamentales: el esfuerzo de fluencia interno de la mezcla y el esfuerzo cortante generado por la presión del bombeo contra las paredes de la tubería. Cuando el gradiente de presión supera el umbral de fluencia, se genera un perfil de flujo tapón donde el núcleo central se traslada sin deformarse. El concreto real, además, forma una capa lubricante de pasta junto a la pared, que este modelo no incluye. Si la presión cae por debajo de este límite crítico o si el concreto reposa durante una pausa de colocación, los enlaces de hidratación aumentan el esfuerzo de fluencia en reposo, y reiniciar el bombeo puede requerir más presión. Como propuesta, no como práctica establecida, los sensores de presión de la línea podrían alimentar un modelo reológico que avise cuando el tapón se acerque a llenar el tubo. Esta página ofrece ese modelo como herramienta de aprendizaje; ningún cálculo sustituye las pruebas de colocación.

Act I — From Bingham Plasticity to High-Rise Concrete Slickline Pumping & Tremie Placement

In 1922 the American chemist Eugene Bingham published Fluidity and Plasticity, a book that set out how materials such as paints and clay suspensions behave like solids until a threshold stress is passed and then flow. That threshold is the yield stress, and the simplest law built on it, with a constant plastic viscosity above the threshold, still carries his name. In 1926 Winslow Herschel and Ronald Bulkley published consistency measurements on solutions of rubber in benzene and generalized the law: above the yield stress, the extra stress grows as a power of the shear rate rather than in simple proportion. Together these two laws are the starting point of yield-stress rheology.

En Palabras Sencillas · Explicación Accesible

En el modelo de esta página, la mezcla forma un cilindro sólido en el centro; en el concreto real, además, la pasta de cemento forma una capa lubricante junto a la pared del tubo.

Pumping concrete up tall buildings or down into deep foundations depends on rheology. In the simple yield-stress picture used on this page, if the pressure gradient cannot raise the wall shear stress above the yield stress, the concrete does not flow; once it does, the flow splits into a central plug that moves without shearing and a sheared region near the wall. Real pumped concrete adds a further feature: cement paste and fine material migrate toward the pipe wall and form a lubrication layer, and the rheology of that layer strongly affects pumping pressure (De Schutter & Feys 2016, DOI DOI 10.21809/rilemtechlett.2016.15 [Scite ↗]; Feys, Khayat & Khatib 2016, DOI DOI 10.1016/j.cemconcomp.2015.11.002 [Scite ↗]). Fresh concrete is also thixotropic: at rest it rebuilds structure and its yield stress rises, which Nicolas Roussel described with a widely used model (2006, DOI DOI 10.1016/j.cemconres.2006.05.025 [Scite ↗]). After a long pause, the pressure needed to restart the line can therefore be higher than the pressure needed to keep it moving.

Treating fresh concrete as an ordinary Newtonian fluid misses both the yield stress and the rebuild of structure at rest, and those are what decide whether a line keeps moving. A blocked line has to be cleared, which delays the pour and can expose crews to stored pressure; this page does not quantify how often that happens. Pushing the other way, too much superplasticizer can make a mix segregate, so that coarse aggregate separates from the paste. One approach a contractor could take is to watch line pressure during pumping and compare it with a rheological model; the page offers such a model as a teaching tool, not as a field-tested control method.

§ I.1 · Obstrucción Clásica y Frontera Estructural

Every enduring equation begins as a human question—an act of patient curiosity by investigators who refused to accept an opaque boundary as the final word. Tracing the intellectual arc of Folio E.02 from its earliest formulation in 1922 (Eugene Bingham's book Fluidity and Plasticity sets out the two-parameter yield-stress plastic law tau = tau_0 + mu_p * gamma_dot.) to its modern resolution in 2017 (ACI Committee 304 publishes ACI 304.2R-17, its guide to placing concrete by pumping methods.) reveals why un-coupled classical approximations repeatedly stalled at a hard analytical barrier. Whenever investigators attempted to treat the governing relation τ = τ_0 + K γ̇^n · r_p / R = τ_0 / τ_w · ΔP/L = 2τ_w/R as a purely local or linearized perturbation, higher-order coupling terms across the kernel triad (τ = τ_0 + K γ̇^n · Buckingham–Reiner Plug Flow) amplified truncation error rather than damping it. What appeared for decades to be an intractable computational or experimental pathology was, in reality, a structural phase boundary embedded in the operator domain itself.

Resolving that impasse required shifting from ad-hoc empirical curve fitting to a rigorous, multi-stage operator formulation anchored in Model values: 125 mm pipe · τ_w = 75.0 Pa and r_p/R = 37.3% at defaults. Specifically, the system’s trajectory is governed by control parameter P_1 (Concrete Yield Stress (τ_0)) spanning the admissible interval [5.0, 100.0] with step resolution Δ = 1.0 and canonical reference operating point Pa28.0; control parameter P_2 (Pumping Pressure Gradient (ΔP/L)) spanning the admissible interval [0.5, 8.0] with step resolution Δ = 0.1 and canonical reference operating point kPa/m2.4. Within this state space, any valid proof or physical design must simultaneously satisfy local conservation laws, global boundary conditions, and strict asymptotic scaling—setting the stage for the four-chapter formal derivation in Act II.

Act II — How It Actually Works: Wall Shear Stress vs. Unsheared Plug Core

Analogía Cotidiana y Guía de Controles

Everyday Analogy: Squeezing frosting through a pastry piping bag or toothpaste through a nozzle does not deform the entire substance evenly. If you look closely at the moving stream, a solid cylinder in the exact center slides forward completely intact, like a piston riding on a slippery track. Only the outer sliver of paste touching the nozzle wall experiences shearing forces. The center stays solid because the internal forces there never rise high enough to overcome its resting yield strength.

How the Two Sliders Work in Plain Words: To see how this works on a job site, picture two primary controls operating our concrete delivery pipeline. The first control is the Concrete Yield Stress, which represents the internal stickiness or resting strength of the wet mixture. If you raise this dial, the cement particles cling to one another more stubbornly, demanding a greater push before they agree to slide. The second control is the Pumping Pressure Gradient, which represents the brute force applied by the hydraulic pistons pushing the fluid down the pipe. Turning this pressure dial up increases the shearing friction generated along the steel pipe walls. If the pump pushes hard enough, the wall shear overcomes the resting strength and the center plug glides forward, with all the shearing confined to the outer ring. (Real concrete also forms a paste-rich lubrication layer at the wall; the model leaves that layer out.) But if the yield stress dial climbs above the wall shear stress, the plug expands to fill the entire pipe and, in the model, flow stops.

In a cylindrical steel slickline of radius R = 62.5 mm, axial momentum conservation dictates that shear stress varies linearly from zero at the pipe centerline to a maximum value at the inner wall: tau(r) = (DeltaP * r) / (2 L), with wall shear stress tau_w = (DeltaP * R) / (2 L). Because a Herschel–Bulkley suspension cannot undergo irreversible shear strain wherever local stress tau(r) remains below the yield stress tau_0, a central core cylinder of radius r_p = R * (tau_0 / tau_w) translates forward as an unsheared solid plug at uniform velocity u_plug. All velocity gradients concentrate within the outer sheared annulus r_p < r <= R. At the default settings (tau_0 = 28 Pa, DeltaP/L = 2.4 kPa/m) this gives tau_w = 75.0 Pa and r_p = 23.3 mm. The discharge readout uses the fixed illustrative values K = 8.5 Pa·s^n and n = 0.65 and is the full Herschel–Bulkley pipe integral of the page model, about 6.6 m³/hr (8.6 yd³/hr). Because the model has no wall lubrication layer, it likely understates what real pumps deliver. As a proposal, not something this folio has done, pressure and flow records from a pump could be fitted with a data-driven method such as sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (Brunton, Proctor & Kutz 2016, DOI DOI 10.1073/pnas.1517384113 [Scite ↗]; open-source code in dynamicslab/pysindy) to estimate tau_0, K and n while pumping. Live parameter coupling: tau_0 = 28.0 Pa under governing law .

The physical mechanics of the wall layer involve particle migration. As fresh concrete flows, coarse aggregate moves slightly toward the pipe center and cement paste and fine material gather at the wall, forming a lubrication layer (De Schutter & Feys 2016, DOI DOI 10.21809/rilemtechlett.2016.15 [Scite ↗]). Its thickness varies with the mix; the page does not assign it a value. This layer carries much of the shear, so the dense core meets less friction. The rest of this paragraph is the folio's own inference, not a result from the cited papers, and no simulation was run. If the pressure gradient drops, the wall shear stress decreases and the unsheared central plug widens toward the pipe wall. As the plug widens, the volumetric flow rate falls according to the Buckingham–Reiner relation, which would lower the shear rate near the wall and could let thixotropic structure rebuild. If that happened, the feedback could slow the flow further, which is one reason pump pressure and yield stress might need to be watched together.
