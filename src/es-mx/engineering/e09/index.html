Act I — Picking Up the Thread from E.01 & E.07: From Roman Pozzolanic Seawalls to Living Self-Healing Concrete

Concrete is strong in compression and weak in tension, so it cracks and lets water and salts reach embedded steel (Van Tittelboom & De Belie 2013, DOI 10.3390/ma6062182). In the first century BCE, Vitruvius described how volcanic ash from the Bay of Naples hardened even under water (De Architectura, Book II). In 2010, Hendrik Jonkers and colleagues tested adding alkali-resistant, spore-forming Bacillus bacteria and calcium lactate to concrete (DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2008.12.036). Water entering a crack could wake dormant spores to precipitate calcium carbonate in the gap (Jonkers et al. 2010).

Capillary suction governed by the Lucas–Washburn law pulls water into a tensile microcrack (Lucas 1918, DOI 10.1007/BF01461107 [Scite ↗]; Washburn 1921, DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.17.273 [Scite ↗]). Jonkers et al. (2010, DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2008.12.036 [Scite ↗]) screened alkali-resistant Bacillus spores as a self-healing additive. Wiktor and Jonkers (2011, DOI 10.1016/j.cemconcomp.2011.03.012 [Scite ↗]) immobilized spores and calcium lactate in porous expanded-clay particles. After 100 days of immersion, bacterial specimens healed cracks up to about 0.46 mm wide, versus 0.18 mm in controls (Wiktor & Jonkers 2011). In an idealized smooth crack, cubic Poiseuille flow scales as w³ (Witherspoon et al. 1980, DOI 10.1029/WR016i006p01016 [Scite ↗]), so a 90% width cut lowers laminar flow 1,000-fold under the stated assumptions. This folio also proposes embedded coda-wave transducers (Snieder 2006, DOI 10.1007/s00024-005-0026-6 [Scite ↗]; Larose & Hall 2009, DOI 10.1121/1.3079771 [Scite ↗]); that pairing is an untested design idea, not a field system.

Replacing cracked concrete carries a direct carbon cost. Global process emissions from cement calcination (excluding kiln fuel) were estimated at 1.50 ± 0.12 Gt CO₂ in 2018, about 4% of global fossil-fuel CO₂ emissions (Andrew 2019, DOI 10.5194/essd-11-1675-2019 [Scite ↗]). In bridges, tunnels, and marine piers, cracks may let chloride ions reach steel bars and drive expansive corrosion (De Muynck et al. 2010, DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2009.02.006 [Scite ↗]). Manual crack injection requires dry access that submerged or buried foundations lack (Van Tittelboom & De Belie 2013, DOI 10.3390/ma6062182 [Scite ↗]). Autonomous sealing could extend service life if field durability holds, though this folio assigns no life-cycle estimate.

The analogy ends at biology, because cement mineral precipitation has no living vascular tissue.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (Lucas–Washburn Capillary Imbibition): A flexural microcrack of width w_0 = 0.30 mm, treated as a parallel-plate channel (r = w_0/2), draws moisture to depth z(t) = √(γ w_0 cos θ · t / 4μ), wetting embedded bacterial spores.
- Stage 2 (Spore Germination & Lactate-to-Calcite Conversion): Alkali-resistant bacteria metabolize calcium lactate, and calcium carbonate precipitates along the crack walls (the mechanism tested by Jonkers et al. 2010).
- Stage 3 (Cubic Parallel-Plate Flow, Q ∝ w³): In this model, narrowing w from 0.30 mm to an illustrative 0.018 mm (94.0% closure) gives (0.018/0.30)³ = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴, a 99.98% cut in idealized laminar flow. Real healed cracks are rough and partly filled, so this is an upper-bound idealization.
- Stage 4 (Ultrasonic Coda-Wave Interferometry): Multiply scattered ultrasonic coda waves are sensitive to very small relative velocity changes Δv/v, so CWI could be used to track stiffness recovery; the 96.4% recovery index shown in the lab is an illustrative model value, not a measurement.
