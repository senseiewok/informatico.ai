At dusk, the insects blinked in disordered, random bursts; but as the night deepened, tiny rhythmic flashes began to synchronize, until whole riverfronts pulsed in simultaneous, blinding illumination without any central leader. Kuramoto recognized that a universal law of non-linear mathematics governed this collective harmony. Half a century later, power systems engineers faced an urgent physical crisis where Kuramoto's insight proved vital. For a hundred years, continental power grids relied on the sheer physical momentum of spinning steel turbines inside coal and hydroelectric stations to keep electrical frequency locked at sixty cycles per second. As power networks transition to solar and battery arrays, that mechanical momentum drops to zero, creating a hair-trigger grid where a sudden cloud shadow can cause catastrophic blackouts.
