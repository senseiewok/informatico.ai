Act I — Straight Boards, Saddle Roofs: How Negative Curvature Built Thin Concrete Shells in Mexico

In post-war Mexico City, building a curved concrete roof usually meant building an expensive curved wooden scaffold first and throwing most of that timber away after the concrete hardened. Félix Candela—a Madrid-born architect who arrived in Mexico in 1939 and co-founded the shell contracting firm Cubiertas Ala—spotted a mathematical escape hatch inside one surface: the hyperbolic paraboloid, or hypar. Although a hypar looks deeply sculpted like a horse's saddle, every point on it lies at the intersection of two completely straight lines. Carpenters could nail ordinary straight pine boards onto simple tilted frames, cast concrete just 40 mm thick across the warped deck, and reuse the boards on the next market hall, factory, or church. This folio pairs Candela's straight-ruled construction insight with Adolf Pucher's 1934 membrane equilibrium formula and examines where the simple interior shear model stops holding near cantilevered edges.

In two companion 1960 ACI Journal papers—General Formulas for Membrane Stresses in Hyperbolic Paraboloidical Shells (DOI 10.14359/8024 [Scite ↗]) and Hyperbolic Paraboloidal Umbrella Shells under Vertical Loads (DOI 10.14359/8026 [Scite ↗]), Candela laid out closed-form equilibrium formulas grounded in Adolf Pucher's 1934 projected stress method. Whereas Folio A.13 traced Heinz Isler's synclastic hanging-cloth shells (curved the same way in both directions, requiring curved molds or pneumatic balloons), anticlastic hypars curve upward along one diagonal and downward along the other. Contemporary structural engineering studies continue to re-evaluate Candela's 8-sided and hexagonal umbrellas (Wang et al. 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.engstruct.2020.111156 [Scite ↗]; Wang et al. 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.engstruct.2022.114577 [Scite ↗]) using modern computational tools alongside nonlinear shell mechanics (Ramm & Mehlhorn 1991, DOI 10.1016/0141-0296(91)90050-M [Scite ↗]).

Flat reinforced-concrete roof slabs carry gravity loads by bending, which forces concrete near the bottom into tension where it cracks unless deep sections and dense steel bars carry the pull. A 200 mm solid flat slab spends a large share of its embodied cement simply holding up its own dead weight. Redirecting vertical gravity into in-plane membrane shear lets a 40.0 mm shell field use 80.0% less concrete volume across the quadrant field than a 200 mm solid slab—before accounting for the central stem, valley ribs, and foundation. At the same time, industrial and civic buildings in heavy seasonal rain need roofs that shed water cleanly without ponding. Knowing both the power and the limits of Pucher's shear formula keeps material-frugal shell design honest.

§ I.1 · Obstrucción Clásica y Frontera Estructural

Every enduring equation begins as a human question—an act of patient curiosity by investigators who refused to accept an opaque boundary as the final word. Tracing the intellectual arc of Folio A.14 from its earliest formulation in 1934 (Austrian engineer Adolf Pucher publishes projected shell equilibrium equations in Beton und Eisen, mapping spatial shell forces onto a flat horizontal reference grid.) to its modern resolution in 2010–2022 (Billington & Garlock (2010, DOI 10.1061/(asce)st.1943-541x.0000144 [Scite ↗]) frame Candela's shells as structural art, while Wang et al. Whenever investigators attempted to treat the governing relation z = (h / (a b)) x y · N_xy = (q_0 a b) / (2 h) · σ_1 = +N_xy / t · σ_2 = -N_xy / t · M_x = M_y = 0 as a purely local or linearized perturbation, higher-order coupling terms across the kernel triad (Doubly-Ruled Hypar Saddle · Pucher Flat-Projection Shear · 45-Degree Principal Arch-Tie Field) amplified truncation error rather than damping it. ## The governing relation
`z = (h / (a b)) x y · N_xy = (q_0 a b) / (2 h) · σ_1 = +N_xy / t · σ_2 = -N_xy / t · M_x = M_y = 0`

## Rules the essay derives
1. Define one quadrant of a hyperbolic paraboloid saddle as z(x, y) = (h / (a * b)) * x * y with twist parameter c = (a * b) / h.
2. Compute Pucher flat-projection membrane shear resultant as N_xy = (q_0 * a * b) / (2 * h) under uniform projected gravity load q_0, noting omitted slope-angle weight amplification.
3. Resolve 45-degree principal stresses as sigma_1 = +N_xy / t (tension) and sigma_2 = -N_xy / t (compression) in the idealized interior membrane model.
4. Evaluate compressive margin lambda_comp = (0.45 * f_ck) / |sigma_2| alongside shell-field thickness savings relative to a 200 mm slab, and flag free-edge bending and buckling limits.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (Doubly-Ruled Hypar Geometry & Unbent Timber Formwork): A hyperbolic paraboloid quadrant z(x, y) = (h / (a b)) x y has negative Gaussian curvature everywhere (an anticlastic saddle) yet contains two families of straight generating lines along constant x and constant y. Because every section parallel to the axes is a straight line, builders could assemble formwork from unbent straight timber planks without curved falsework (Cassinello et al. 2010, DOI 10.3989/ic.10.040; Garlock & Billington 2011, DOI 10.2749/101686611x13049248220564).
- Stage 2 (Pucher Projected Membrane Equilibrium): Under uniform plan-projected gravity load q_0 = 1.741 kN/m² (40.0 mm concrete at ρ = 2,400 kg/m³ giving 0.941 kN/m² plus 0.80 kN/m² superimposed load), Pucher's 1934 projected equilibrium equation cancels normal membrane terms because ∂²z/∂x² = 0 and ∂²z/∂y² = 0 identically. In this idealized interior membrane model with supported edges, N_x = N_y = M_x = M_y = 0 and uniform shear becomes N_xy = (q_0 a b) / (2 h) = 31.35 kN/m.
- Stage 3 (Diagonal Principal Arch-Tie Field & Thickness Ratio): Rotating 45 degrees onto the bisecting diagonals resolves pure shear into equal principal tension σ_1 = +N_xy / t = +0.784 MPa and principal compression σ_2 = -N_xy / t = -0.784 MPa. Against an allowable compressive threshold of 0.45 f_ck = 15.75 MPa (f_ck = 35 MPa), the model compressive ratio is λ_comp = 20.1, while the 40 mm shell field uses 80.0% less concrete volume than a 200 mm solid flat slab (excluding stem and ribs).
- Stage 4 (Central Stormwater Funnel & Tier T2 Model Boundary): Grouping four quadrants into a 24.0 m by 24.0 m (576 m²) umbrella slopes rainwater inward toward a central hollow stem, collecting 8.0 L/s (28.8 m³/h) under 50 mm/h design rain. Hypothesis (Tier T2 analytical model): straight-ruled anticlastic curvature carries symmetric gravity load primarily in in-plane shear rather than bending. This flat-projection model is falsified for physical cantilevered umbrellas where slope-angle self-weight growth, free-edge boundary bending, valley-rib concentration, concrete creep, or thin-shell buckling (Ramm & Mehlhorn 1991, DOI 10.1016/0141-0296(91)90050-M; Wang et al. 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.engstruct.2020.111156) govern failure.

def evaluate_candela_hypar(

    semi_span_b_m: float = 12.0,
    corner_rise_h_m: float = 4.0,
    thickness_mm: float = 40.0,

        semi_span_a_m <= 0.0
        or semi_span_b_m <= 0.0
