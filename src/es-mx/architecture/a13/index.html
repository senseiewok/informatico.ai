Act I — Hanging Cloth in Winter Air: How Physical Models Found Shapes That Equations Missed

In September 1959, at the first International Association for Shell Structures congress in Madrid, a young Swiss engineer named Heinz Isler stepped up with a brief lecture titled 'New Shapes for Shells' (published in the IASS Bulletin in 1961). While senior builders debated cylindrical barrels and circular domes derived from textbook formulas, Isler argued that gravity and surface tension could solve three-dimensional shell equilibrium without a single closed-form equation. He showed three physical ways to find forms: molding hills of earth or plaster, inflating rubber membranes or expansion foam, and hanging wet cloth or latex sheets from fixed anchor points until they settled under self-weight, stiffening them—sometimes by letting wet fabric freeze outdoors in Swiss winter air—and turning the resulting pouch upside down. This folio pairs Isler's experimental lineage with the classical elastic buckling theory of spherical shells and modern imperfection-sensitivity research.

En Palabras Sencillas · Explicación Accesible

En palabras sencillas, imaginen sostener una sábana húmeda por sus cuatro esquinas. La gravedad la curva en una hamaca suave donde cada hilo está estirado en tracción. Si esa forma se endurece y se voltea boca arriba, las trayectorias que estaban en tracción pasan a trabajar en compresión limpia hacia los apoyos. Así buscó Heinz Isler la geometría de sus cubiertas delgadas de concreto. No obstante, al hacer un techo tan esbelto como el cascarón de un huevo a gran escala, el riesgo principal no es que el concreto se aplaste bajo su propio peso, sino que una pequeña hendidura hacia adentro provoque un pandeo repentino mucho antes de agotar la resistencia del material.

Isler's Madrid paper closed with a grid of thirty-nine freeform shell sketches followed by 'etc.', signaling that physical equilibrium opened an unbounded family of structural forms (Tang 2015, DOI 10.1260/0266-3511.30.1.1 [Scite ↗]). Over the next four decades in Burgdorf, Isler engineered more than a thousand thin-shell canopies across Europe, including the iconic Deitingen Süd motorway service station (1968), whose two triangular concrete wings span 31.6 m while resting on just three supports each. As Gabriel Tang (2015, DOI 10.1260/0266-3511.30.1.1 [Scite ↗]) documents, Deitingen's 90 mm thickness was governed primarily by placing 20 mm of protective concrete cover over two layers of steel reinforcement rather than by raw compressive strength. Later computational mechanics—reviewed by Ekkehard Ramm and Gerhard Mehlhorn (1991, DOI 10.1016/0141-0296(91)90050-M [Scite ↗]) bridged physical hanging models to finite-element shape optimization and nonlinear ultimate-load analysis tracking concrete cracking, creep, and large deformations. More recently, Tang and Pedreschi (2020, DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)AE.1943-5568.0000430 [Scite ↗]) explored pairing curved timber gridshells as reusable formwork for thin concrete shells to lower labor and falsework barriers.

Conventional flat concrete slabs carry floor and roof loads through bending, placing half the cross-section in tension and requiring deep slabs and heavy steel reinforcement. Doubling the span of a flat beam raises bending moments by a factor of four, so long-span slabs spend most of their capacity carrying their own dead weight. Curved funicular shells redirect vertical loads into in-plane membrane compression, allowing a 90 mm concrete shell thickness (an illustrative 80% thickness ratio relative to a 450 mm solid flat slab, before counting edge beams or supports) to bridge wide spans. Understanding when thin shells fail by elastic snap-through buckling rather than crushing is therefore central to designing light, material-frugal roofs safely.

# Heinz Isler's Funicular Thin-Shell Domes: Inverted-Hanging Form-Finding, Zoelly Buckling & Imperfection Knockdowns (Folio A.13)

A plain-text companion to the essay at https://informatico.ai/architecture/a13/ (`A.13 · Heinz Isler Funicular Thin-Shell Domes & Imperfection Knockdowns · REV 1.0`, `REV 1.0`, redated `2026-11-20`).

## The governing relation
`R = (L² + 4f²)/(8f) · σ_mem = q R / (2t) · p_cr^cl = 2E / √(3(1−ν²)) · (t/R)² · p_cr^act = γ_kd · p_cr^cl`

## Rules the essay derives
1. Compute spherical-cap curvature radius R = (L^2 + 4 * f^2) / (8 * f) and uniform-projection crown membrane stress sigma_mem = (q * R) / (2 * t); record the rim slope factor sec(phi_0) omitted by uniform plan projection.
2. Compute Zoelly (1915) classical elastic buckling pressure p_cr^cl = (2 * E / sqrt(3 * (1 - nu^2))) * (t / R)^2 and verify quadratic (t/R)^2 scaling when thickness doubles.
3. Apply an explicit empirical-style knockdown function gamma_kd(R/t) = 0.14 + 0.32 * exp(-0.1 * sqrt(R / t)) as an illustrative bound (not a code rule) and check that thin 15 mm caps drop below lambda_b = 3.0.

## How it works, in four stages
- Stage 1 (Inverted-Hanging Cloth & Pneumatic Form-Finding): In his 1959 IASS Madrid lecture (published 1961), Swiss engineer Heinz Isler grouped thin concrete shell form-finding into three physical experiments: freely shaped hills, inflated pneumatic membranes, and hanging textiles stiffened with plaster or frozen in winter air and inverted. Because a hanging sheet carries gravity load in pure membrane tension, its inverted rigid geometry channels uniform dead load along compression-only trajectories with minimal bending.
- Stage 2 (Idealized Spherical-Cap Geometry & Uniform-Projection Membrane Stress): As a tractable analytical reference—distinct from Deitingen Süd's three-point free-edge wing shells, which share only the 90 mm double-rebar-cover thickness—the model evaluates a spherical cap of span L = 31.6 m and rise f = 4.2 m (curvature radius R = 31.82 m, rim half-angle φ_0 = 29.8°). At t = 90.0 mm (ρ = 2,400 kg/m³) plus 1.20 kN/m² superimposed load, uniform plan-projected gravity load is q = 3.318 kN/m² (neglecting the sec φ_0 = 1.152 slope factor at the rim and boundary haunches), giving crown compressive stress σ_mem = 0.587 MPa.
- Stage 3 (Zoelly Classical Elastic Buckling & Post-Buckling Sensitivity): Because σ_mem = 0.587 MPa is under 2% of an assumed 35 MPa concrete compressive strength, crushing does not govern this model. Instead, thin doubly curved shells face elastic snap-through buckling. R. Zoelly's 1915 linear-elastic formula for an ideal uniform sphere under normal pressure gives p_cr^cl = 282.9 kN/m² at E = 30.0 GPa and ν = 0.20—an idealized upper bound that omits concrete creep, cracking (Ramm & Mehlhorn 1991, DOI 10.1016/0141-0296(91)90050-M), asymmetric snow, and edge bending.
- Stage 4 (Illustrative Knockdown Curve & Tier T2 Hypothesis): Hutchinson (1967, DOI 10.1115/1.3607667), Gerasimidis et al. (2018, DOI 10.1115/1.4040455), and Abbasi, Derveni, and Reis (2023, DOI 10.1115/1.4056801) analyzed why tiny inward dimples sharply cut spherical shell buckling capacity below Zoelly's line. Applying an illustrative empirical-style knockdown function γ_kd = 0.14 + 0.32 exp(−0.1 √(R/t)) (NASA SP-8007 and Dulácska inspired, not a design code formula) at R/t = 353.5 yields γ_kd = 0.189, p_cr^act = 53.4 kN/m², and buckling ratio λ_b = 16.1. Hypothesis (Tier T2 model): elastic snap-through under inward dimples governs reserve rather than crushing; falsified for a physical shell if creep, cracking, asymmetric snow, or deeper inward dimples (δ_0 / t > 0.5) push collapse below λ_b = 3.0 or induce edge bending.

      #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Independent offline verifier for Folio A.13 (Isler-Inspired Thin Spherical Cap Buckling Model).

Standard library only. Recomputes the idealized spherical-cap geometry, uniform-projection
membrane stress, Zoelly (1915) classical elastic buckling pressure, and an illustrative
empirical-style imperfection knockdown curve inspired by NASA SP-8007 and Dulacska.

Usage:
  python skills/architecture/a13/verify_a13_isler_buckling.py
  python skills/architecture/a13/verify_a13_isler_buckling.py --planted-defect
  python skills/architecture/a13/verify_a13_isler_buckling.py --planted-defect=no-knockdown

Note: These are model calculations for an idealized spherical cap, not a structural code check
or engineering sign-off for any physical building.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import json
import math
import re
import sys
from pathlib import Path

ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[3]
PAGES = (
    ROOT / "src" / "architecture" / "a13" / "index.html",
    ROOT / "src" / "es-mx" / "architecture" / "a13" / "index.html",
)

_DEFECT_ARG = next((a for a in sys.argv[1:] if a.startswith("--planted-defect")), "")
DEFECT = (_DEFECT_ARG.split("=", 1)[1] if "=" in _DEFECT_ARG else "linear-scaling") if _DEFECT_ARG else ""


def spherical_cap_radius_m(span_m: float, rise_m: float) -> float:
    return (span_m ** 2 + 4.0 * (rise_m ** 2)) / (8.0 * rise_m)


def zoelly_classical_pressure_kn_m2(
    elastic_modulus_gpa: float,
    poisson_ratio: float,
    thickness_m: float,
    radius_m: float,
) -> float:
    c_factor = 1.0 / math.sqrt(3.0 * (1.0 - poisson_ratio ** 2))
    e_kpa = elastic_modulus_gpa * 1e6
    ratio = thickness_m / radius_m
    exponent = 1 if DEFECT == "linear-scaling" else 2
    return 2.0 * c_factor * e_kpa * (ratio ** exponent)


def illustrative_knockdown_gamma(radius_m: float, thickness_m: float) -> float:
    if DEFECT == "no-knockdown":
        return 1.0
    r_over_t = radius_m / thickness_m
    return 0.14 + 0.32 * math.exp(-0.1 * math.sqrt(r_over_t))


def evaluate_isler_spherical_cap(
    span_m: float = 31.6,
    rise_m: float = 4.2,
    thickness_mm: float = 90.0,
    elastic_modulus_gpa: float = 30.0,
    poisson_ratio: float = 0.20,
    concrete_density_kg_m3: float = 2400.0,
    superimposed_load_kn_m2: float = 1.20,
) -> dict[str, object]:
    t_m = thickness_mm / 1000.0
    radius_m = spherical_cap_radius_m(span_m, rise_m)
    phi0_rad = math.asin((span_m / 2.0) / radius_m)
    rim_secant = 1.0 / math.cos(phi0_rad)

    q_sw_kn_m2 = (concrete_density_kg_m3 * 9.80665 * t_m) / 1000.0
    q_total_kn_m2 = q_sw_kn_m2 + superimposed_load_kn_m2
    sigma_mem_mpa = ((q_total_kn_m2 * radius_m) / (2.0 * t_m)) / 1000.0

    p_cl_kn_m2 = zoelly_classical_pressure_kn_m2(
        elastic_modulus_gpa, poisson_ratio, t_m, radius_m
    )
    gamma_kd = illustrative_knockdown_gamma(radius_m, t_m)
    p_act_kn_m2 = gamma_kd * p_cl_kn_m2
    lambda_b = p_act_kn_m2 / q_total_kn_m2

    return {
        "span_m": round(span_m, 2),
        "rise_m": round(rise_m, 2),
        "thickness_mm": round(thickness_mm, 1),
        "radius_m": round(radius_m, 2),
        "edge_half_angle_deg": round(math.degrees(phi0_rad), 1),
        "rim_secant": round(rim_secant, 3),
        "q_sw_kn_m2": round(q_sw_kn_m2, 3),
        "q_total_kn_m2": round(q_total_kn_m2, 3),
        "membrane_stress_mpa": round(sigma_mem_mpa, 3),
        "classical_buckling_kpa": round(p_cl_kn_m2, 1),
        "knockdown_gamma": round(gamma_kd, 3),
        "actual_buckling_kpa": round(p_act_kn_m2, 1),
        "buckling_ratio_lambda": round(lambda_b, 1),
        "thickness_ratio_vs_450mm_pct": round((1.0 - t_m / 0.45) * 100.0, 1),
    }


def check_html_readouts(ref: dict[str, object]) -> list[str]:
    errs: list[str] = []
    expected_tokens = (
        f"{ref['membrane_stress_mpa']:.3f} MPa",
        f"{ref['classical_buckling_kpa']:.1f} kN/m²",
        f"{ref['knockdown_gamma']:.3f}",
        f"{ref['actual_buckling_kpa']:.1f} kN/m²",
        f"{ref['buckling_ratio_lambda']:.1f}",
    )
    for page in PAGES:
        if not page.is_file():
            continue
        txt = page.read_text(encoding="utf-8")
        strip = re.search(r'<div class="tp-readout-strip".*?</div>\s*</div>', txt, re.S)
        scope = strip.group(0) if strip else txt
        for tok in expected_tokens:
            if tok not in scope:
                errs.append(f"{page.relative_to(ROOT).as_posix()} missing readout token {tok!r}")
    return errs


def main() -> int:
    ref = evaluate_isler_spherical_cap()
    thin_neg = evaluate_isler_spherical_cap(thickness_mm=15.0)
    t60 = evaluate_isler_spherical_cap(thickness_mm=60.0)
    t120 = evaluate_isler_spherical_cap(thickness_mm=120.0)

    failures: list[str] = []

    if ref["radius_m"] != 31.82:
        failures.append(f"Expected R=31.82 m, got {ref['radius_m']}")
    if ref["membrane_stress_mpa"] != 0.587:
        failures.append(f"Expected sigma_mem=0.587 MPa, got {ref['membrane_stress_mpa']}")
    if ref["classical_buckling_kpa"] != 282.9:
        failures.append(f"Expected p_cl=282.9 kN/m^2, got {ref['classical_buckling_kpa']}")
    if ref["knockdown_gamma"] != 0.189:
        failures.append(f"Expected gamma_kd=0.189, got {ref['knockdown_gamma']}")
    if ref["actual_buckling_kpa"] != 53.4:
        failures.append(f"Expected p_act=53.4 kN/m^2, got {ref['actual_buckling_kpa']}")
    if ref["buckling_ratio_lambda"] != 16.1:
        failures.append(f"Expected lambda_b=16.1, got {ref['buckling_ratio_lambda']}")

    # Quadratic (t/R)^2 scaling invariant: doubling thickness from 60 mm to 120 mm must quadruple p_cl.
    scale_ratio = float(t120["classical_buckling_kpa"]) / max(float(t60["classical_buckling_kpa"]), 1e-9)
    if abs(scale_ratio - 4.0) > 0.05:
        failures.append(f"Zoelly quadratic thickness scaling violated: 120mm/60mm ratio={scale_ratio:.3f} != 4.0")

    # Monotonicity: thinner shells (higher R/t) must have strictly lower knockdown factor gamma_kd.
    if not (float(thin_neg["knockdown_gamma"]) < float(t60["knockdown_gamma"]) < float(ref["knockdown_gamma"])):
        failures.append("Knockdown monotonicity violated: thinner shells did not receive lower gamma_kd")

    # Negative control: 15 mm shell at R=31.82 m must drop below lambda_b = 3.0.
    if float(thin_neg["buckling_ratio_lambda"]) >= 3.0:
        failures.append(
            f"Negative control failed: 15 mm shell stayed at lambda_b={thin_neg['buckling_ratio_lambda']} >= 3.0"
        )

    failures.extend(check_html_readouts(ref))

    ok = len(failures) == 0
    payload = {
        "status": "PASS" if ok else "FAIL",
        "folio_id": "A.13",
        "planted_defect": DEFECT or None,
        "reference_90mm": ref,
        "negative_control_15mm": thin_neg,
        "zoelly_doubling_ratio": round(scale_ratio, 3),
        "failures": failures,
    }
    sys.stdout.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8")
    print(json.dumps(payload, indent=2, ensure_ascii=False))
    return 0 if ok else 1


if __name__ == "__main__":
    raise SystemExit(main())
